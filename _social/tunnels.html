When I was in high school, there were rumors about underground tunnels beneath the school.

And one night, my friends and I actually found them.

Teachers used to warn students never to enter the tunnels. There were even stories about students getting lost down there. Every so often, a teacher would hint at where some of the entrances were, but my friends and I ended up discovering a way in that we’d never heard anyone talk about.

It happened one night when a few of us were out walking around town, as we often did back then. Eventually, we wandered past the high school. It was late, the building was dark, and nobody was around.

But as we passed one of the doors, we noticed something strange.

It was unlocked.

Being the curious teenagers we were, we went inside.

There’s something unsettling about being in a massive school in the middle of the night. The hallways were dimly lit, everything was quiet, and the building felt completely different without hundreds of students filling it.

But we weren’t particularly concerned. We wandered the halls, stuffed ourselves into lockers, laughed, and generally acted like the ridiculous teenagers we were.

That is, until we came across a room that looked like a janitor’s closet. The door was wide open, the light was on, and hanging on the wall just inside the doorway was a ring of keys.

Well, the temptation for a little adventure was apparently too much for us to resist.

We took the keys and started exploring. Eventually, we discovered that one of them fit the lock on an old elevator. And the interesting thing about this particular elevator was that it only went down.

Naturally, we decided the best thing to do was cram all of ourselves into this tiny, ancient elevator and see where it took us.

So we turned the key.

The elevator creaked to life, and we started descending beneath the school. I honestly couldn’t tell you how far down we went. But I remember those elevator doors opening.

Right in front of us were the tunnels.

Long concrete corridors stretching off into darkness. The same tunnels we’d spent years hearing rumors about. The ones teachers warned us to stay out of. The ones students supposedly got lost in.

And somehow, we’d actually found them.

I remember standing there looking down those corridors, and suddenly the whole adventure didn’t seem quite as exciting anymore.

None of us knew how far the tunnels went, where they led, or what was down there. And something about staring into the dark made us realize that maybe we didn’t actually want to know.

We looked at each other, and we all seemed to have the same thought.

Nope. We’d seen enough.

So we rode back up to the main floor, returned the keys to the janitor’s closet, which was somehow still sitting wide open, and then left the school like nothing had happened.

That old high school has since been torn down, and I have no idea what happened to those tunnels or whether any part of them still exists.

But every once in a while, I think about that night.

Part of me wishes we’d been a little braver. We’d heard about those tunnels for years, and there we were, standing right at the entrance with nothing stopping us from exploring them.

But another part of me is glad we turned around.

Because even after all these years, I can still remember what it felt like when those elevator doors opened. Just standing there, looking into those long, dark concrete corridors, knowing they extended somewhere beneath the school, but having absolutely no idea where.

We found the tunnels. We proved the rumors were true.

But we never found out where they led.

And now that the school is gone, I wonder if we ever will.
