My last post caused some issues I didn’t intend, so I want to clarify a few things. I never said no one offered help. I saw deep compassion from people during that time, and I’m truly grateful for that.

But I still stand by my decision to stay in a shelter instead of with family. While shelter life had its challenges and uncomfortable moments, it provided something invaluable: privacy, independence, professional support, resources, and structure that I couldn’t have found elsewhere. Living with family can sometimes lead to tension, crossed boundaries, or burned bridges, and by choosing the shelter, I was able to avoid that and focus on rebuilding.

The experience taught me that homelessness isn’t the personal failure I once believed it was. I thought staying in a shelter meant I had failed as a parent and as a person, but that’s simply not true. Shelters are community resources designed to help people stabilize and rebuild, and that’s exactly what happened for us. I don’t regret choosing that path, and I’m grateful we had access to it.

My original post wasn’t meant to seek pity or cause conflict. It was meant to process my experience, to educate others about the realities of homelessness, and to challenge the stigma that still surrounds it. I shared it to tell the truth about a journey that, in the end, led to growth and stability.

I don’t know if this post will change anything. Some people will probably still find fault in my words, no matter how carefully I try to say them. But I’m just trying to be honest about my experiences—because I’ve always been this way. Maybe it makes me naïve, but I’ve never been good at hiding how I feel. I’ve always been willing to set aside my pride and say the things most people wouldn’t dare say out loud. That honesty gets me in trouble sometimes, but I don’t know how to be any other way. If being vulnerable helps even one person feel less alone, then maybe the discomfort is worth it.
