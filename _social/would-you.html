Would you lay down your life
    for the one you love?

It is an easy hypothetical.
A swift and romantic surrender.

We make promises of sudden, violent ends:
“I would take the fall.”
“I would step into the fire.”
“I would burn the world to the ground
    and stand in the ashes with your name on my lips,
        just for you.”

And perhaps,
    in the wreckage,
        that looks like devotion.

Maybe there is something beautiful
about loving someone enough
to surrender everything in
    one final,
        holy second.

But I don’t want your ghost.

I am asking for something
    infinitely
        harder.

I am asking you to live.

To keep breathing
even when grief settles inside you
like still water.

To pry open your eyes
when the gravity in your bones
begs you to sink.
    To drag the curtains apart.
    To let the sun touch your face,
    even when you don’t know
    what to do with the light.
Would you keep choosing morning?
Even as the dark whispers your name,
promises you quiet,
promises you nothingness?

Can you find one
    single
        kind
            thing
inside a day that tried to ruin you?

Dying is a reflex.
Living
    is a relentless,
    agonizing
        choice.

It takes the brutal, ticking hours
and asks you to endure them.
        On purpose.

To sit shivering inside the pain,
refusing to build a home in its hollows.
To stop pretending that erasing yourself
is the same thing as finding peace.

To believe
—against all the screaming evidence in your head—
that there is still something
    Worth wanting.
    Worth touching.
    Worth seeing.

I know you would lay it down.
You would drop it in a heartbeat.

    But would you pick it back up?

Would you linger
    just long enough
    to watch the night sky turn to gold?

Would you let me love you
past the exact moment
you decided you were beyond saving?

    Would you live?

Not because it is easy.
(We both know it isn’t.)

But because
    I love you enough
        to ask.
