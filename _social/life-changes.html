I’m sorry I’ve been keeping to myself lately.

I’m coming to the end of my leave of absence, and the thought of returning to work has been bringing up some pretty strong emotions.

A lot of it, if I’m being honest, is dread.

Don’t get me wrong. I love being a nurse. I care deeply about people, and I genuinely want to make a positive difference in their lives. But lately, I can’t shake the feeling that I’m just another cog in the healthcare machine.

And I want more than that for my life.

I believe deeply in advocacy. I’m passionate about autonomy, consent, accountability, and helping people reclaim control over their own lives. I also believe that people who commit crimes, particularly those involving abuse and exploitation, should be held accountable for their actions.

Lately, I’ve been putting a lot of my time and energy into building software designed to collect and organize evidence of potentially illegal activity. I’ve already used some of this work to submit evidence to the FBI, and I’ve written to members of Congress about gaps I’ve identified in existing laws that I believe need to be addressed, particularly when it comes to sexual abuse and exploitation.

I’m intentionally being vague about some of the details because there are things I’m not ready to share publicly yet.

But the more I work on this, the more I realize that what I’m building could potentially serve a much broader purpose.

I want to explore ways to help survivors of domestic violence navigate the reporting process, document evidence, understand their legal options, and pursue accountability through the justice system. I want survivors to have access to information and resources that help them regain control over their lives, rather than feeling powerless against the very systems that are supposed to protect them.

I know how complicated these systems are, and I understand that meaningful change doesn’t happen overnight. But I also know what it feels like to need help, to want justice, and to realize how difficult it can be to navigate everything on your own.

Maybe I’ll get nowhere. Maybe nothing will come of any of this. Maybe I’ll spend years trying to change something that refuses to change.

But I have to at least try.

Because I don’t want to spend the rest of my life wishing I’d done something when I had the opportunity to make real change.
