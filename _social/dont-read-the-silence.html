For anyone who cares about my books… I FINALLY finished my complete rewrite of The Silence, and the second edition is officially live on Amazon. 😭

I feel like I finally turned it into the story I always meant for it to be. Only took me rewriting practically the entire damn book.

“Mileena, but is The Silence something I’d even WANT to read?”

Excellent question.

Honestly, I’m terrified of people actually reading my books, so rather than trying to convince you to buy it, I’ve decided to provide a comprehensive list of reasons you absolutely should NOT read The Silence.

You’re welcome.

  1. You don’t like fantasy?

Don’t even THINK about picking up this book. You’ll be subjected to extensive worldbuilding, ancient gods, fictional civilizations, religious institutions, political conflicts, and an interconnected history of Arcadia that I’ve spent an unreasonable amount of my life developing.

God forbid a fictional world have a functioning society.

  1. You want a fast-paced adventure that gets straight to the point?

STOP RIGHT THERE.

This is a slow-burning BISH. You’re going to meet five different protagonists, learn about their lives, understand their trauma, and watch their individual storylines gradually collide.

Yes, there are epic battles. Yes, there are monsters. Yes, things get progressively more unhinged.

But first, you’re going to get emotionally invested in these fictional people whether you like it or not.

  1. You enjoy cozy, comforting fantasy?

Absolutely not.

We have graphic violence, body horror, psychological horror, cosmic horror, religious persecution, political violence, and some deeply disturbing supernatural nonsense.

There's also a god who thinks the solution to suffering is to simply yeet all of existence into oblivion.

Very reasonable. Very mentally stable.

  1. You hate poetry and dramatic descriptions?

Oh, you’re going to HATE me.

I have an unfortunate habit of describing absolutely horrific things with beautiful, poetic language.

Is someone experiencing unimaginable suffering? Great. Let me describe the lighting, the weather, the blood, and the delicate way the flowers move beside their dying body.

We’re making trauma ✨aesthetic✨.

  1. You need completely happy endings?

HAHAHAHA.

Listen, I love my characters.

Unfortunately, loving them has never stopped me from putting them through absolute hell.

There is hope. There is love. There is triumph.

There are also consequences.

Nobody gets to defeat an ancient god and then magically pretend the last 500 pages didn’t happen.

  1. You prefer short books?

Well, this particular book is THICC.

546 pages.

FIVE HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIX.

You’re moving to Arcadia for a while. Pack accordingly.

  1. You prefer your villains to be straightforward evil people?

Sorry.

Some of mine genuinely believe they’re doing the right thing.

Which is incredibly inconvenient because now we have to discuss morality, grief, religious certainty, and whether good intentions actually excuse doing absolutely horrific things.

Spoiler: they don’t.

  1. You don’t like complicated magic systems or divine mythology?

Yeah, no.

I’ve got an entire pantheon, divine bloodlines, ancient wars, forgotten histories, magical artifacts, and theological disagreements.

Some of the people studying these things are also spectacularly wrong.

Turns out being a scholar doesn’t automatically make you correct. Who knew?

  1. You don’t enjoy becoming emotionally attached to fictional characters?

Then please stay away.

Because I took the time to give these people friendships, personalities, childhood memories, hopes, fears, and ordinary little habits.

You might start caring about them.

And that sounds like a terrible idea considering what kind of book this is.

  1. You prefer books that don’t make you question your entire existence?

Probably skip this one too.

Because underneath all the gods, monsters, politics, violence, and general chaos, the book is essentially asking whether life is worth living when suffering is unavoidable.

You know.

Just some light recreational reading.

10/10 would not recommend if you’re looking for a relaxing afternoon.

Anyway, there are probably more reasons not to read it, but I’m trying not to be too negative.

But if all that sounds like your kind of thing, the second edition is finally available on Amazon. 🤪
