I’m autistic. I’m weird and unusual. And that’s something I’ve learned to accept as my truth.

I’ve always known I wasn’t quite like the people around me. Since childhood, I’ve been trying to answer three questions: Who am I? Why am I feeling this way? And how do people actually see me?

I have over 14 years of data on myself: journal entries, poems, daily mood reviews, song lyrics, dream journals, esoteric ritual documentation with observations, analytical research papers on my own psychology, social media posts, transcriptions of conversations, and much more. What started as handwritten journals has turned into GitHub repositories meticulously catalogued for future reference.

And when I love someone, when I truly lock in on a person, I take that entire system and aim it at them.

Because to me, understanding someone is the highest form of intimacy.

I want to know what makes my person hurt, so I’m never the cause of that pain. I want to know what makes them feel safe, so I can understand why they flinch and make sure I’m never the reason they do again. I want to know what makes them light up, so I can do those things on purpose instead of by accident.

That’s how I love. Not with grand gestures, but with attention, with data, with the quiet, relentless act of studying someone until I know them better than they know themselves, and then using everything I’ve learned to take care of them.

Most people love with feelings first and figure the rest out later. I love with understanding first, because feelings without understanding are just guessing. And I don’t want to guess with someone’s heart.

I think what I’m really trying to say is that people want to be known. Truly, deeply, specifically known. Recognition is one of the most powerful things we can offer another human being. And I believe the more we understand ourselves honestly and rigorously, even when the answers are ugly, the more we can understand the people we love. And the more we understand the people we love, the more we can understand the strangers carrying the same things in silence.

And that’s how we make a better world. Not by pretending we’re all okay, but by getting brave enough to figure out why we’re not, and then handing that understanding to someone who needed the blueprint.
