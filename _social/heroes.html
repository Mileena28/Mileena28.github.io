The ones most acquainted with suffering are often those most capable of extraordinary mercy.

For having felt the brutal hand of pain upon their own soul, they stand forever at a crossroads: to become hardened by what wounded them, or to grow gentle in defiance of it. And those who choose kindness after cruelty carry a rare and sacred strength.

For having crawled through their own living hell, they learn how to bear light into the dark places where others still wander. For having loved deeply and lost dearly, they know the sacred worth of what is meant to be kept, and they do not handle it with careless hands.

Your suffering was never your due.

Yet in surviving it, in rising from it as a warrior rather than a ruin, you were given a solemn gift: to protect, to guide, to guard what is fragile, and to become a blessing where once there was only harm.

May we all bring forth the gifts carved into us by sorrow, and offer them freely to a world in need.
