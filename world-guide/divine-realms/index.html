Divine Realms

Sacred domains where divine beings dwell and cosmic power flows unconstrained

Hall of Eternal Judgment

Cosmic Government Seat

The crystalline amphitheater where the assembled gods hear cases and pass judgment. Crystalline thrones arranged in ascending tiers catch and refract light that has no earthly source, creating patterns that hurt mortal eyes to perceive; the First Gods sit highest, and the younger gods cluster about their thrones like courtiers around a queen. The air shimmers with power barely contained, and a First God's laughter can shake the very foundations of the celestial realm. It had not echoed with divine footsteps for centuries when the Divine War began—until a god came before it with a case that trembled the hall with the weight of assembled gods.

Trace the Divine History

Celestial Gardens

Senia's Domain of Creation

Senia's domain exists in perpetual golden light where reality unfolds into impossible beauty. Flowers bloom in fractals of pure radiance, each petal containing entire worlds; trees of crystallized sunshine sway in breezes that carry the music of distant stars. The very air seems alive with possibility, and the sun goddess moves among celestial flowers that bloom and wither at her passing, cloaked in a radiance that would obliterate mortal sight.

Meet Senia

Twilight Boundary

Neutral Ground Between Realms

The boundary between realms where day surrenders to night. Neither sun nor moon rules here completely: twilight creates a neutral ground on which opposites—silver and gold, night and day, tide and flame—can exist together without dimming each other's essence, completing rather than canceling one another. It was here that the god of the moon and the goddess of the sun walked together, visible only to a seer's magically enhanced sight.

Meet Elodias, God of the Moon

The Deepest Trenches

Where the Leviathans Dream

The depths beneath the Azure Expanse, where light has never penetrated and the leviathans swim in the deepest trenches, dreaming dreams that would shatter mortal minds. Their bones remember the deep, the dark before light, and being gods; and when the cosmic balance is disturbed, ancient creatures stir in the deepest currents, sensing it.

Sail the Azure Expanse

River of First Light

Primordial Waters of Creation

The primordial location where humanity was shaped from responsive clay. This cosmic waterway ran with a color that had no name—somewhere between dawn and dream, between hope and memory—its banks soft with dust fine as ground pearls that had never known footsteps. Each handful of that dust spoke of potential; each grain whispered of becoming; and there Thainos, light itself given consciousness, worked not as a god commanding matter but as an artist conversing with clay.

Meet Thainos, Who Shaped the Clay

The Deep Places

Wild Gods' Exile Sanctuaries

The Wild Gods who survived the war did not ascend to some distant plane. They buried themselves in the deep places of the world—in caves that echo with unspoken words, in forests where no mortal foot has trod for millennia—and made themselves into living tombs, conscious and aware but forever separate. What remains of them speaks only in sacred spaces where divine words can be contained, and even there their voices are muffled, careful, wrapped in so many layers of restraint that hearing them requires a lifetime of preparation. The rest are dead or in hiding.

Remember the Great Withdrawal

The Pantheon

The gods whose loves, judgments, and silences shaped Arcadia—each with a fuller account among the Characters

The First Gods

Those who awakened in the void before creation and spoke reality into being: Leika, Mother of Mountains, who raised the mountains from her breath and taught stone to reach skyward; Libros the Lawgiver, living mathematics, who inscribed the laws that hold reality in place; Archadeus, who gave the hunt its hunger and the predator its purpose; Atonia, who distilled truth from chaos into beauty that cuts like winter wind; and Verites, who from the spaces between light and substance whispered darkness into being—not as absence but as presence, as necessary as breath. Libros's angels, Archadeus's beastmen, and Atonia's elves are their handiwork. They view mortals as mayflies, and it was they who set their brother Thainos his thousand-year test.

Meet the Mother of Mountains

Thainos, the God Who Wept

The god who shaped humanity from responsive clay on the banks of the River of First Light, giving them softer skin than made sense, hearts that could break from mere words, and souls that could contain infinities. Challenged by his siblings to walk among his creations stripped of immortality, he searched a thousand years for one human untouched by cruelty. The Wanderer constellation marks the moment his search ended and his essence scattered like seeds across all humanity—the Scattered Divinity every mortal now carries.

Meet Thainos

Elodias and Senia

Elodias, god of the moon and tides, and Senia, the sun incarnate, goddess of warmth and beauty and growing things—bound by a curse woven from moonlight, shadow and blood by the jealous seer Malritha. Senia reincarnates endlessly as a mortal with no memory; Elodias retains perfect divine recall; and when he finds her she has seven days after remembering before her mortal form dissolves. The Lovers constellation formed the instant he chose eternal cycles of love and loss over never seeing her again. Senia's domain is the Celestial Gardens.

Read Seven Days of Sun

Azrael, the Fallen Guardian

God of Guardian Angels, whose angels protect mortals but do not walk the earth—until the day he himself chose mercy over divine law, killed his corrupted brother, and was cast from heaven. Walking the earth bearing the weight of necessary sin, he found in a small forest village that redemption comes not from divine forgiveness but from the stubborn love of those who see past the monster to the man: Rynn, and the impossible child Hope.

Meet Azrael

Silas, the Trickster

The Trickster God, of oil-slick eyes and silver hair, shifting form and claws disguised as nails. It was Silas who offered divine blood to a grieving mortal named Astrid Ravenscroft and so became the architect of the Bloodmarked—the gift that taught mortals divinity could die, and the first thread of the Divine War.

Meet the Trickster

Khali, the Dream Shepherd

The ancient Dream Shepherd, who chose to walk among mortals as a simple healer in a cottage where the river knew her name though she had never told it. When she caught the eye of both a foreign prince, Eshuah, and a jealous princess, Jade, divine love became the catalyst for mortal tragedy—and gold rewrote what followed into a mural of a radiant Vaelari striking down a shadowed figure glowing with ethereal darkness. Her true story is told in The Song of Blood and Stone.

Meet Khali

Zenith and Solarius

Zenith is named God of Gods in accounts known to the characters, while Solarian tradition associates Solarius with the kingdom and the Crown of Aetheris. The crown’s power is demonstrated in The Silence; the account of a Sun God’s spark remains an attributed origin story. The elves of Sommer Isle keep what they call the purest worship of Zenith, and in the years after the Silence a new faith proclaimed him the one true God and every other deity a demon. Mordain’s research also attributes the Eclipse Pendant to Zenith; the novel does not independently verify its maker.

Walk the Ruins of Solara

The Silent God

A consciousness older than memory, dormant since before the dawn of the divine war itself: a deity whose name was erased from all but the most sacred texts, whose temples crumbled to dust, whose existence became a whispered heresy. Not a god of thunder or fire, earth or water, but of the void itself—the silence before creation, the stillness after destruction. Its awakening came not with a roar but with whispers, and its purpose was not dominion or worship but the unmaking of existence. Its stolen vessel met its end at a hunter's blade in the ruins of Solara, yet the Awakener constellation burns eternal as a warning.

Hear the Whispers

Divine Hierarchy & Politics

The cosmic governmental systems that determine the fate of gods and mortals alike

The First Gods

The architect-gods who awakened in the void before creation, sang substance from the void, and inscribed the laws that hold reality in place; their combined presence is a weight that could crush mountains and rewrite the laws of reality itself. To their court mortals are mayflies—brief creatures whose individual lives matter less than maintaining cosmic order and divine supremacy. Their apparent cruelty, the historian Tanneus G'Raja came to believe, masks a desperate restraint learned through catastrophe.

Study the Ancients

The Wild Gods

The faction of deities who believe that mortals should be able to walk among divinity without being preyed upon—that divine power should protect rather than exploit. They walked among mortals as friends, protectors, and guides, and heard every desperate plea. Their love, expressed without the restraint that keeps the infinite from overwhelming the finite, began cascading consequences—divine words reshaping existence, blessings becoming curses—that ended in the Divine War, reality unraveling, and their withdrawal to the deep places. Their whispers still reach the Dawnseekers of the Verdant Veil, they bestow blessings upon anyone, even those who do not follow their will, and in the Hollow Spire's forbidden texts their vibrant creation stands opposed to the Silent God's stillness. To Zenith's Chosen they are false idols and demons.

Remember the Fallen

Cosmic Government

In the Hall of Eternal Judgment the gods present cases, make accusations, and render judgment. The First Gods occupy the highest thrones, while the younger gods cluster around them like courtiers—beings of sharp, crystalline beauty who ask why they should concern themselves with creatures that live for barely a century, and who deem that mortals unable to adapt to new predators deserve extinction.

Meet the Lawgiver

The Elemental Angels

Accounts studied at the Hollow Spire describe angelic powers and invocation. In The Silence, the Angel of Light directly answers Mordain’s call: it appears in white garments, with black eyes and red pupils, and bears a sword of white energy. Mordain willingly offers his existence and disappears. The Light drives back the depicted Wraiths and closes the palace opening. Later, Ryx and Kaelen show memory gaps, while Mordain’s belongings remain. The encounter does not establish a universal price for every invocation or reveal the fate of his soul. In the story: The Silence, Chapters 25 and 41–44.

Study the Six Elements

The Silence as Mercy

The central mystery of mortal theology—why don't the gods answer?—has an answer the historian Tanneus G'Raja called poison. The gods are not absent, deaf, or indifferent: they hold themselves back with a restraint so absolute it looks like abandonment, because they learned through apocalypse that divine words reshape existence, that every blessing becomes a curse in time, and that love expressed directly from the infinite to the finite does not elevate but incinerates. Their cruelty was kindness; their distance was love. “Pray they never love us enough to speak again.”

Gods of Other Shores

Divinities from works still in development—premise-level only, as the author has published them; names, domains and details may change

The Dragon (The Song of the Dragon — planned trilogy)

The most ancient and mysterious of the constellations appeared when the First Gods discovered something that predated them—something so terrible they built it a prison among the stars. What lies imprisoned is lost to memory, but since its inception all who grieve have heard the same song in their sorrow, and within its notes some claim to hear promises of paradise unlocked, of death undone, of reunion with all that was lost. The Song of the Dragon tells how, in a distant age, the scholar Eren Glavier loses his beloved Raewyn to plague, follows a map hidden in the song's verses, and breaks the seal imprisoning The Dragon—an entity with the power to resurrect anything that has ever died. Raewyn breathes again, but The Dragon's freedom begins resurrecting the Old Gods who hunger for vengeance.

Read the Dragon's Prophecy

The Three Gods of Hevetica (The Senach — in development)

Across the sea lies the Imperial Republic of Hevetica, a nation of towering, long-lived Bohdkin whose three gods created them and never left them. Ordinary Bohdkin are blessed—their weapons carry god-granted magic and their nation runs on divine favor—but only the seers, the state's oracle class, are infused with divine magic through a ritual of devotion, and only to the seers do the gods speak. Where Arcadian divinity is ancestry carried in the blood, Bohdkin divinity is borrowed rather than born. Certain their order is superior, they call an Arcadian a Senach—godless savage. The names and domains of the three gods, and where they stand in the ancient schism between First Gods and Wild Gods, are questions the author has not yet answered.
