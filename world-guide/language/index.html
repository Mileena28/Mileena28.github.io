Languages of Arcadia

Bae'Ravah · Naru · the Common Tongue · Bohd

Arcadia speaks in many voices. Most of the mainland trades and quarrels in the Common Tongue; the oldest folk of the island of Nareen still speak flowing, compositional Naru; and beneath them both lies Bae'Ravah, "The Ancient Wisdom Tongue" — the lost language of the original Baelak, a sacred tongue of balance, trauma, healing, and spiritual understanding that modern beastmen have all but forgotten. Across the sea, the Bohdkin of Hevetica speak Bohd. Bae'Ravah — The Ancient Wisdom Tongue

Bae'Ravah [BAY-rah-vah] is the lost language of the original Baelak — the divine beastmen so rare that most Arcadians count them a myth within a myth. Its first speakers called themselves the Fus'Ravahn, "Children of the Divine," an ancient feline beastman people who are now gone; the cat (kata) was sacred to them as their base form. Modern beastmen have forgotten this sacred tongue, and it survives today only in the keeping of the last of its ancient speakers — a story for a later book of The Chosen Chronicles.

The language emphasizes concepts of balance, trauma, healing, and spiritual understanding, and it prizes directness over grammatical complexity. It has no direct equivalent for "the": the article is implied by context and added only in translation to the Common Tongue. Core Philosophy

Bae'Ravah prioritizes directness and simplicity over grammatical complexity. Meaning comes from precise vocabulary rather than elaborate sentence structures. The language reflects the Fus'Ravahn value of honest, straightforward communication.

Basic Sentence Structure

Standard Word Order: Subject + Verb + Object + Time/Place

Bae'Ravah: "Vek frish kara hor'sinta" → English: "I walk home today"


Questions: Question word + Subject + Verb + Object

Bae'Ravah: "Qua dak gish?" → English: "What do you make?"

Basic Grammar Rules

1. Verb System - NO CONJUGATION

Verbs never change form regardless of subject or tense. Time is shown with separate time words.

English Bae'Ravah
I walkVek frish
You walkDak frish
They walkKelth frish
I walked yesterdayVek frish tor'sinta
I will walk tomorrowVek shevek frish zen'sinta
I am walkingVek frish nal
2. Plurality - USE QUANTITY WORDS

Nouns never change form. Quantity is shown with number or quantity words.

English Bae'Ravah
One childUni bafu
Many childrenManu bafu
Some childrenSevek bafu
Twenty peopleDu'tona opa
All enemiesKoral raka'vul
3. Possession - PRONOUN + NOUN

Simple juxtaposition - no possessive markers or changes to words.

English Bae'Ravah
My painVek rikta
Your strengthDak braj'va
Their homeKelth kara
The warrior's bladeMok'tunak swix
4. Comparisons - MOOKA/LOCHE/EQUAS STRUCTURE

Use comparison words for all comparisons. No comparative/superlative word changes.

English Bae'Ravah
You are stronger than meDak erza mooka vek
This is more beautifulTis mira mooka
The most dangerous enemyRaka'vul bav'glek mooka koral
As strong asErza equas
Less painfulRikta loche
5. Articles - CONTEXT DETERMINES

"The" is optional and shown by context or "eth" when emphasis needed.

English Bae'Ravah
The houseKara (or "eth kara" for emphasis)
A houseKara
HousesManu kara
6. Negation - KETH BEFORE VERB

"Keth" negates any verb or adjective.

English Bae'Ravah
I do not walkVek keth frish
You are not strongDak keth erza
They will not comeKelth keth shevek doveka

Time Expressions

Tense Through Time Words

Special Grammar Notes

Cultural Speech Patterns

  • Drop articles when possible: "Pain teaches wisdom" not "The pain teaches the wisdom"
  • Use present tense for universal truths: "Rikta gish braj'va" (Pain makes strength)
  • Emphasize through word choice, not grammar: Use precise vocabulary rather than complex structures

Emotional Intensity Through Repetition

  • Strong emphasis: "Gaks gaks gaks!" (gossip gossip gossip!)
  • Certainty: "Y'lu y'lu!" (yes yes!)
  • Dismissal: "Unk unk!" (no no!)

Quick Reference Guide

Remember:

  1. No verb conjugations - same verb form always
  2. No noun pluralization - use quantity words
  3. Simple possession - pronoun + noun
  4. Time through separate words - not verb changes
  5. Negation with "keth" before what's being negated
  6. Comparisons with mooka/loche/equas - no word modifications
  7. Cultural concepts may need explanation - shimah, zhak'mor, vala'dak tum have no direct English equivalents
  8. Context matters - some words like "tora" have multiple meanings
  9. Preserve emotional weight - don't simplify profound concepts

The Arcane Calendar

The Bae'Ravah calendar terms follow the ancient Arcane Calendar, the reckoning of divine mathematics that Atonia first carved from pure logic. Each period is named for the elemental and divine forces that govern time itself: the calendar runs on 8-day weeks representing the eight primary cosmic forces, and twelve months of 32 days each, for a perfectly divisible year of 384 days. Dates are spoken as day name, day number and month name — Kaia'sinta mor septa sul Zhakaran, "Spiritsday, the 7th of Sangrien."

Naru — The Old Tongue of Nareen

On Nareen, the island southwest of Arcadia, most people now speak the Common Tongue — the trade language everyone learns for dealing with travelers and merchants from other lands — but the oldest among them still speak Naru, the ancient native language of their people. Naru is characterized by flowing, water-like sounds and compound words that build meaning through combination. The language reflects the Nareenese worldview: fluid, interconnected, and deeply rooted in nature.

Naru is a highly compositional language. Complex concepts are built by combining simpler root words, reflecting the Nareenese belief that all things are connected and built from fundamental elements. When words are joined, no letters are cut or shortened from either part — the only exception is a repeated letter at the junction, which is written once. So seru (large) and mai (water) become serumai, the ocean; and Nareen itself is an orunahoikaremai — land surrounded by water.

Naru Grammar

1. Sentence Structure - SUBJECT, VERB, OBJECT

Naru follows Subject-Verb-Object word order. Questions open with a question word.

Naru English
Yi era te wamamai.I see the river.
Ishla hura wo matsu.She dances with love.
Te leo sei ervu.The sun is warm.
Wera yu levasa?Where do you go?
Tima festua koma?When does the festival come?
Qi sora tera?Why do tears fall?
2. "Sei" - THE UNIVERSAL "TO BE"

Sei serves as "am," "is" and "are" for every person. Negation is simply noh (not) placed before the word it denies.

Naru English
Yi sei osha.I am lost.
Matsu sei monea.Love is sacred.
Usa sei tokamu.We are together.
Yima namu sei Amina.My name is Amina.
Yi noh nesua kesika yu.I do not want to hurt you.
Tat noh sei pontua.That is not an accident.
3. Plurals - ADD "S"

Plurals are formed by adding s to the end of a word.

Singular Plural
Doma (person)Domas (people)
Romala (flower)Romalas (flowers)
Starra (star)Starras (stars)
Cor (eye)Cors (eyes)
4. Verb Tense - NO CONJUGATION

Naru verbs do not change for tense. Timing is carried by context, by time words (toa now, toma today, yemasa yesterday, tomasa tomorrow, ferta later), or by the future-intention marker wia (will). Optional suffixes can add precision, but the Nareenese prefer context and flow over rigid grammar.

Naru English
Yi caru mai.I carry water / I carried water / I will carry water (timing from context)
Yi caru mai toa.I carry water now.
Yi wia caru mai tomasa.I will carry water tomorrow.
Le nimo pata yemasa.He walked the path yesterday.
Yi caruna mai yemasa.I carried water yesterday (suffix -na emphasizes completion).
Yi carusey mai toa.I am carrying water now (suffix -sey emphasizes ongoing action).
5. Suffixes - LAYERS OF MEANING

Naru suffixes are optional tools for poets, storytellers, ceremony and emotional emphasis rather than everyday speech. They may be stacked, in the order verb tense, then continuous, then other modifiers: huranaseychialah, "was dancing in a cute, sacred way."

Suffix Meaning Example
-seyContinuous, ongoing actionWama (flow) → Wamasey (is flowing)
-naPast, completed actionMatsu (love) → Matsuna (loved)
-roOne who does something (casually, as a trait)Hura (dance) → Huraro (a dancer)
-weroProfession, occupationTesa (teach) → Tesawero (teacher)
-chiDiminutive, small, dearChino (child) → Chinochi (little child, baby)
-lohWithout, lackingMai (water) → Mailoh (waterless, dry)
-shiExcessive, too muchWama (flow) → Wamashi (overflowing, flooding)
-alahSacred, blessedPata (path) → Pataalah (sacred path)
-komaBecoming, turning intoNias (dark) → Niaskoma (darkening)
-toaJust, recentlyCom (come) → Comtoa (just arrived)

The Common Tongue (Arcadian)

The Common Tongue is the trade language used throughout most human settlements of Arcadia — the speech everyone learns for dealing with travelers and merchants from other lands, and the tongue into which Bae'Ravah and Naru are rendered when they are translated. Even on far Nareen most people now speak it, keeping Naru for their elders. Arcadia's linguistic landscape reflects the complex relationship between divine and mortal realms, and the Common Tongue carries the marks of it: divine loanwords borrowed from celestial languages for concepts that do not exist in purely human experience; a habit of euphemism that avoids the direct names of dangerous supernatural entities; regional dialects that incorporate local magical traditions and environmental influences; formal registers reserved for ceremony, official occasions and legal proceedings; and subtle methods for folding hidden meanings into everyday speech.

Sacred and Non-Human Tongues

Beside the Common Tongue stand the ancient languages used for magical and religious purposes. Old Celestial is the language of formal divine communication, known only to advanced temple scholars; the Elemental Tongues are aligned with natural forces and used for environmental magic; Blood Speech is a forbidden form that spends life force to empower words; Dream Language functions chiefly in unconscious states; and Void Whispers are dangerous forms of speech that can summon entities from cosmic emptiness. Arcadians have also devised means of reaching what is not human — the deep harmonic patterns of Leviathan resonance that can touch an ancient sea god's consciousness, the visual and scent-based signalling of the Beastborn, the written Angel Script kept for correspondence with divine messengers, the gestural and symbolic codes of the nomadic Free People, and the mediation techniques used to speak with the dead.

Bohd — The Language of Hevetica

From The Senach, a series in development; details may change. Across the sea, the towering, long-lived Bohdkin of the Imperial Republic of Hevetica speak Bohd. Only one word of it is yet fixed in canon, and it is a slur: Senach, the Bohdkin word for an Arcadian, meaning literally "godless savage." The Bohdkin mock Arcadians for having been abandoned by their gods — our gods would never leave us — and when Hevetica comes to govern Arcadia, the Bohd language comes with it, imposed by edict. What "Bohdkin" and "Hevetica" themselves mean in Bohd, and the shape of the language's grammar, remain to be revealed.
