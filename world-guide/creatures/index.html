Creatures & Beings

"From benevolent Nimwe to corrupted Beastborn, witness the entities that inhabit a world where divine blood transforms innocence into nightmare"

In Arcadia divine power leaves nothing it touches unchanged. Here dwell beings born from divine accident and mortal transformation, creatures that remember what they once were, spirits that still tend the wild places their makers abandoned, and creatures of the void that walk between worlds. Each represents a different facet of how divine power interacts with the mortal realm—sometimes as blessing, often as curse, always as fundamental change.

Benevolent Entities

Guardians of Balance and Hope

These beings emerge from the void not as destroyers but as healers, seeking to restore balance to a world increasingly dominated by unchecked light—and beside them endure the small, stubborn spirits of stone and stream that the Wild Gods left behind. They offer guidance without demanding worship, healing without extracting payment, and hope without requiring surrender of free will.

The Nimwe

Void-Touched Spirits

Creatures of the void who dwell in the deep woods and walk between worlds, witnessing the rise and fall of light and shadow, of gods and mortals. They appear as impossibly tall, inhumanly graceful women with skin pale as sculpted moonlight, hair like liquid silver, delicate white cat-like ears, a long tail thick with pristine fur, and luminous violet eyes that are windows into the fathomless void; the void itself clings to them like a living shroud, and they can take the shape of small white cats. Their voice is layered into a harmonious chord, as if the forest itself had spoken, and they call troubled souls “little starling.” Their whispers carry prophecy meant to warn and prepare, never to control. They arose in numbers after the Silence, first in the forests around Rivenglade, to restore a balance tipping toward unchecked light—which is why Zenith's Chosen brand them demons and forbid the forest.

Prophetic Whispers Void Affinity Shapeshifting Spiritual Healing
Silver Feather Tokens
When a Nimwe departs she leaves behind an impossibly delicate, luminous silver feather that pulses faintly with otherworldly energy. The token is proof and prophecy in one, and it hums with power in the presence of those able to sense magic—which makes it as dangerous to keep as it is precious. Their teaching is simple: too much light will make you blind.

Spirits of the Wild

Leika's Children

Believed by the oldest texts to be the children of Leika, the Mother of Mountains—who breathed life into stone and stream and gave consciousness to tree and glade—the nature spirits remain present in the world in ways their creators no longer are. A water sprite stands eight to ten inches tall, chubby and translucent blue-green with silvery highlights, its enormous eyes holding points of light like reflected stars, a mane of living water flowing around its neck independent of any breeze; such a spirit will keep a village well clean for thirty years in exchange for bread and honey cakes, and cannot resist a silver coin. Forest nymphs, the dryad-kin, are barely larger than a human child, with moss-colored skin, hair like woven leaves, fingers like delicate twigs, and a chirping language of their own. In the Verdant Veil the Dawnseekers commune with such spirits, and sacred animals bear divine markings.

Elemental Bond Offerings & Bargains Dryad Language Fading Memory
Voices of Warning
Before the Silence, spirits across the realms began forgetting their purposes; one water sprite went suddenly still and spoke in a hollow, distant voice: “The Silence… It's coming.” Their behaviour is a barometer of the world's health, and the subject of the scholar Alaquinn Althaea's research journal, Voices of the Wild.

Ancient Powers

Elder Beings of the Deep and the Heavens

Beings older than the kingdoms of mortals: leviathans that remember the dark before light, the Titans whose bones became the land, the ordered hosts of angels that serve creation's balance, and the sirens of far Nareen.

Ancient Leviathans

Primordial Sea Gods

Colossal sea creatures of the deepest trenches of the Azure Expanse, dreaming dreams that would shatter mortal minds. Their bones remember: “They sing,” said the seer Malritha, her hand pressed to the bleached ribs of one that had beached itself the night of her birth. “They remember the deep. They remember the dark before light. They remember being gods.” When cosmic balance is disturbed, ancient creatures stir in the deepest currents, and leviathan bone trembles in silent recognition, remembering its kin.

Deepest Trenches Shattering Dreams Bone That Remembers Once Gods
The Beached Leviathan
The Weaver of Sorrows tells of a leviathan that beached itself and lay decaying on the shore for three days while no scavenger would touch it; the moon temple claimed the skeleton as an omen and displayed its ribs for centuries—until a child harvested them for a loom whose frame pulsed with faint internal light, as if the creature's spirit remained trapped within its bones.

The Titans

Bones of the World

Colossal beings of an elder age whose remains form the very landscape of Arcadia. The Hollow Spire, fortress-city of the Chroniclers of Bone, is carved from the bleached skeleton of a fallen Titan; mountains are said to have formed from the ribs of titans; and on the ancient god-battlefields of the Crimson Tundra, crumbling structures and colossal statue fragments jut from crimson snow like the skeletal remains of enormous beasts, on ground where gods clashed and titans fell. How they came to fall is a matter for the Lost Histories.

Fallen Colossi Bone Cities Elder Age Buried Memory
The Hollow Spire
Scholars and mystics carved their libraries into the bones of the fallen—the Hollow Spire, where forbidden texts hint at gods yet to awaken, and where dungeons are hewn from the Titan's very bones.

Angels

Servants of Order and Light

Where Thainos's humans know the chaos of longing, the angels of Libros the Lawgiver know only perfect order, singing in a harmony that has never once chosen to rebel and return. Under Azrael, God of Guardian Angels, the guardian angels protect mortals but do not walk the earth—until Azrael himself chose mercy over divine law and was cast from heaven. Greater still are the angels that govern each of the six elements, primordial forces of creation and balance. The Angel of Light, when invoked, appears clothed in seamless white garments of celestial fire, with skin like polished diamond, void-black eyes with burning red pupils, and a sword of pure white energy—and it grants its aid only in exchange for the supplicant's complete erasure from existence.

Perfect Order Guardian Angels Elemental Dominion Terrible Beauty
The Fallen Guardian
The Sin of Mercy tells how Azrael killed his corrupted brother and walked the earth bearing the weight of necessary sin; The Silence tells what one repentant inquisitor paid to summon the Angel of Light against the void.

Sirens of the Deep

Sea-Kin of Nareen

On the island nation of Nareen—far from Arcadia's shores—the sirens of the sea share a sacred covenant with the Nareenese who live alongside them. After storms the people leave offerings of fish upon the rocks, and in return the sirens allow their magical artifacts to wash upon the beach for mortal use, and leave water from the deepest trenches, black sand from the ocean floor, and great shells found only in the deep. A siren that washes ashore dead must be buried on the beach in a tomb of seashells so that its soul may return to the sea. Those few whose hair turns white are recognised as blessed and can understand the sirens' language, serving as bridges between the two peoples.

Sacred Covenant Sea-Gifts Bone Prophecy Shell Tombs
The Siren Bone Oracle
In Arcadia, siren bones—“gifts from depths no living thing should visit”—were the needles through which Athea, the blind oracle of the Great Withdrawal, touched the night sky and wove her calendar, tarot and horoscopes. Nareen's lore belongs to the companion novel The Girl Beneath the Cherry Tree, still in progress.

Corrupted Beings

Innocence Transformed by Divine Violence

These creatures represent the ongoing consequences of cosmic violence, transformed by exposure to divine blood corruption or birthed by the encroaching silence—innocent beasts made monstrous through no sin save being in the wrong place when divinity bled, their bodies remembering shapes they should not possess.

The Beastborn

Divinely Corrupted Animals

Born where the spilled blood of warring gods pooled in the earth—manifestations of the gods' wrath, sorrow, and forgotten desires—the Beastborn are animals rewritten by divine essence into horrific amalgamations of their original forms: wolves the size of horses, cougars grown extra eyes and venomous claws, birds whose wingspans blot out the sun. They multiply through contamination rather than breeding, moving with the particular wrongness of bodies remembering shapes they should not possess. Some carry deadly toxins that only magic can heal. Bloodmarked who overuse their power risk becoming Beastborn themselves, driven by the insatiable will of the old gods.

Contamination Enhanced Hunting
Major Classifications
Ridgeback Stalkers (armored ambush predators of the Wastes), Gorefang Behemoths (massive tusked chargers), Razorwing Harpies (canyon-spanning flyers whose screech can shatter stone), Stonehide Titans (earth-shaking giants), Skywraiths (circling terrors of the sky), Wasteland Crawlers (whose leathery hides make hunters' armor), the Ice Boars of the tundra, and the terrifying Virelith—shadow panthers with hollow sockets for eyes and tendrils of inky darkness that devour light itself. During the Silence, Void Wraiths led Beastborn hordes; void-corrupted Beastborn still haunt the wilds, hunted by wandering protectors.

Void Wraiths

Harbingers of Silence

Abominations birthed from the encroaching silence, black as the utter absence of light. Taller than any man, their silhouettes are vaguely humanoid yet utterly alien, their flesh a swirling, viscous darkness that drinks in the light and leaves a palpable chill in sun-drenched air. Two points of malevolent white light burn within the featureless void of the head, and at the center of the chest flickers a symbol: a black void encircled by a faint, shimmering ring of light, like a solar eclipse frozen in eternal silence. With skeletal fingers of pure blackness they drain a victim's essence as sickly black mist until the body implodes—not blood and gore, but complete erasure. The Silent God tears them from rents in reality as extensions of its own will, and their sole directive is to unmake.

Life Drain Silence Emanation Reality Consumption
Post-Crisis Status
With the defeat of the Silent God, the Void Wraiths vanished as smoke in the wind and the encroaching silence receded like a dying tide. Yet the terror seeped into the collective consciousness, leaving a residue of unease and a lingering fear of the unseen and the unknowable, and blighted regions near the epicenter of the void's influence remain twisted and leached of life.

Hybrid Bloodlines

Children of Two Worlds

When divine blood meets mortal flesh through union rather than combat, it produces hybrid beings—living consequences of the catastrophic meeting of infinite power and finite flesh, neither wholly human nor wholly divine, embodying the dangerous poetry of divine longing made flesh and touching both shores while belonging to neither.

Vaelari

Celestial-Elven Hybrids

Elf-divine hybrids, exceedingly rare and almost legendary, born when celestial desire meets elven grace. They are marked by intensely vibrant light blue eyes—“eyes that hold starlight,” eyes touched by the heavens—a universally recognised hallmark of the lineage. Their births are seen as significant omens, inspiring awe and reverence within both elven and human societies and particularly among the spiritual elite. Most commonly found on Sommer Isle in the Azure Expanse, they drift through mist-veiled shores like walking poetry, beautiful and terrible in their otherworldly power.

Eyes That Hold Starlight Omen Births Sommer Isle Walking Poetry
Raised with Reverence
A Vaelari raised on Sommer Isle is met from birth with immense reverence and expectation, seen as divinely touched—yet the Vaelari are not native to the isle and remain rare even there. Their blood, one prince's grandmother said, touches both shores but belongs to neither.

Haelim

Divine-Human Hybrids

Divine-human hybrids scattered across the mainland and hidden among mortal populations, with eyes that see too much and hearts that burn too bright for mortal flesh to contain. They are living secrets, their divine heritage betrayed only by the weight of eternity in their gaze.

Hidden Among Us Eyes That See Too Much Living Secrets Weight of Eternity
Hidden Among Mortals
Such a child is neither wholly human nor wholly divine—too powerful for mortal flesh, too wild for mortal society—and its power may wake before it can walk. The Sin of Mercy tells of one such child, Hope—proof that divine and mortal blood can coexist when raised with love instead of violence.

Baelak

Divine Beastmen (Mythological)

Beastman-divine hybrids so rare that most consider them myth within myth—legends told by drunkards and madmen. Of all the children of divine longing they are the rarest, and the least is known of them.

Beastman Blood Divine Touch Myth Within Myth Rarest of All
Legendary Rarity
So rare that most consider them mythological rather than real beings, the Baelak spring from the same terrible source as their Vaelari and Haelim cousins: the meeting of infinite power and finite flesh.

Transformed Beings

Mortals Reshaped by Supernatural Forces

Mortals who have been changed by divine blood—granted godlike power at the cost, a little at a time, of their original humanity.

The Bloodmarked

Divine Blood Warriors

The rare mortals in whom divine essence works a different metamorphosis than the Beastborn's: beings capable of wielding godlike power while forever balanced on the knife's edge of monstrous corruption. The Lost Histories trace the mark to the Divine War, when the trickster god Silas offered his blood to a grieving mortal named Astrid Ravenscroft—the first Bloodmarked, and the first to prove that divinity could die—and over generations the blood came to run in family lines. Each Bloodmarked carries a blessing tied to their godly lineage, whether of Body, Mind, or Spirit—a monstrous transformation of fur, claw and fang triggered by extreme emotion or threat; command over growing things; controlled divine fire; a lion's shape; a mind that bends other minds—and each carries a curse that tempts toward madness. Every transformation erodes their humanity a little further.

Divine Combat Abilities God-Killing Power Involuntary Transformation Hereditary Power
Champions or Curse
The songs remember them as monsters and the Ashborn exile those who show the mark; the Bloodfang Clans sacrifice their Bloodmarked children to the War God; Solara is ruled by Bloodmarked nobles who wield controlled divine fire; the Dawnseekers revere theirs. And in the forbidden depths of the Hollow Spire, the oldest texts remember the Bloodmarked not as abominations but as champions raised to stand against the void.

Peoples of Arcadia

The Three Races and Their Neighbours

For millennia three races—humanity, the ethereal Elfkind, and the brutish Beastmen—have lived beneath the divine shadow, building civilizations atop ancient battlefields and raising kingdoms that reflect forgotten prayers. The oldest texts say each was shaped by a different First God, and that the youngest of them, humanity, was made deliberately fragile.

Humanity

Thainos's Clay

Shaped from responsive clay on the banks of the River of First Light by Thainos, the god who wept. Where Libros's angels knew only perfect order, these creatures would know the chaos of longing; he gave them softer skin than made sense, bodies that would bruise at a touch, hearts that could break from mere words, minds that would never be satisfied, and souls that could contain infinities housed in flesh that lasted mere moments. He made them absurd. He made them glorious. From the relic-hunting Ashborn of the Godscarred Wastes to the fire-blooded nobles of Solara, the fishing folk of Atheria and the refugees of Rivenglade, humanity has built the greater part of Arcadia's kingdoms.

Free Will Fragile Flesh Scattered Divinity Endless Reaching
The Wanderer's Legacy
When Thainos completed his thousand-year search for one soul untouched by cruelty, his essence scattered like seeds across all humanity—making every human a potential carrier of sacred light.

Elfkind

Atonia's Children

The ethereal Elfkind, whom the First God Atonia shaped to know crystalline truth. The reclusive Dawnseekers of the Verdant Veil live in symbiosis with a forest made sentient by the blood of the Wild Gods, communing with spirits, hearing the whispers of the Wild Gods, and revering their Bloodmarked, whose powers are tied to nature itself. Far to the southeast, the elves of Sommer Isle raise cities of white marble and gold filigree devoted almost exclusively to Zenith; proud, reserved, and secure in their own purity, they regard the Dawnseekers as “low elves” tainted by the wider world.

Nature Communion Long Memory Pointed Grace Divided Faiths
The Verdant Veil
The Dawnseekers guard the Verdant Veil, a realm where trees whisper forgotten prayers, rivers shift course with moods, and beasts bear divine sigils—and where its princess, Elowen Faerwyn, was raised to speak the rustling language of the leaves.

Beastmen

Archadeus's Children

A race of highly intelligent animals who stand on their hind legs, whom the First God Archadeus shaped to know primal strength. Each species forms its own tribe—feline, ursine, and more—and in the Crimson Tundra they are united under the Bloodfang Clans, whose brutal laws, fierce loyalty, and veneration of the War God demand strength, sacrifice, and ferocity. Any Bloodmarked born into the clans is deemed a sacred offering, ritually slaughtered on the Blood Moon so that warriors may drink the divine blood and be enhanced. Not all Beastmen accept these ways: exiles from the tundra can be found as far away as the Hollow Spire's own acolytes.

Primal Strength Tribal Loyalty Night Senses War God's Rites
The Stray
Ryx Shadowclaw, a feline cub who fled the sacrificial fate his Bloodmarked gift decreed and can take the shape of a massive black lion with crimson eyes, is the best-known son of the Crimson Tundra.

Murmeleks

Gossip Brokers

Half-goat, half-human creatures who serve as the realm's gossip brokers and information dealers. They trade in secrets, selling news and rumor to the highest bidder, which makes them generally disliked despite their usefulness—to be compared to a Murmelek is to be accused of peddling secrets. Through them, news and scandal travel across the world, making Murmeleks valuable allies or dangerous enemies depending entirely on who pays their price.

Information Trade Mercenary Loyalty Wide Networks Goat-Kin
Everything Has a Price
A Murmelek's loyalty lasts exactly as long as the coin that bought it—a fact known to everyone who has ever tried to keep a secret in Arcadia.
