Religious Organizations

In the absence of divine voices, mortal institutions interpret silence as scripture, transforming withdrawal into worship and abandonment into absolute truth.

Zenith's Chosen

Monotheistic Religious Movement
A religious order that rose in the refugee haven of Rivenglade five years after the Silence, when Lucien Altheris—a Vaelari of radiant beauty and hypnotic voice, the self-proclaimed "Son of Zenith"—arrived preaching salvation through absolute devotion to a single god. Zenith is the one true Creator, the source of light, order, and truth; the Wild Gods are false idols or demonic deceptions; the Silence was the prophesied end times, and the blighted present a tribulation before Zenith's purified reign, which only the baptized will survive. The Son is Zenith's infallible voice on earth, and prophecy promises his ascension to godhood. Beneath him serve the Devout (regional Overseers), the Faithful (local Elders), and the baptized Chosen, honored with the "preaching work" of spreading the Light, alongside Messengers of the Light sent abroad. Expansion combines conversion with practical aid—food, order, restoration work in battered Atheria—while control runs through mandatory sermons, dress codes, family members reporting one another for signs of doubt, a forbidden forest, scriptures that reinterpret the fragments of the Hollow Spire, and the brutal Forsaking of dissidents. The Nimwe—the benevolent void-spirits who arose after the Silence—are branded demonic tempters, and contact with them means Forsaking or worse.
Primary Base: Rivenglade (missions to Atheria and the Wastes)
Leadership: Lucien Altheris, "The Son of Zenith"
Methods: Conversion, Social Control, Persecution
Influence: Growing rapidly post-Silence
Zenithian Doctrine Baptism Rituals The Forsaking Devout · Faithful · Chosen

The Temples

Traditional Religious Communities
Religious communities organized around the service of divine beings. In the Age of Gods the ancient moon temples were kept by lines of seers who chanted hymns unchanged in ten thousand years; it was in such a temple that the lunar seer Malritha built her loom of leviathan bone and wove moonlight into fate, and it was a temple that claimed a beached leviathan's skeleton as an omen. Since the Great Withdrawal, it is in sacred spaces alone—those few sanctuaries where divine words can be contained—that what remains of the withdrawn gods still whispers, in voices so muffled and careful that hearing them requires a lifetime of preparation. In The Silence, the Temple of Solara remains standing amid the damaged city. Its roof is intact, its walls smoke-stained, and a fallen lamp obstructs one door (Chapter 42).
Seers: Guardians of the ancient moon temples
Sanctuaries: Where the withdrawn gods still whisper
Notable: Temple of Solara (standing amid the damaged city)
Moon Temples Sacred Sanctuaries Temple of Solara

Underground & Resistance

In hidden sanctuaries and forgotten places, the cast-out and the truth-seekers build new societies from the fragments of what was lost.

The Forsaken Communities

Underground Exile Networks
Those who break Zenith's rules—forbidden love, questioning the leadership, dissent—or who leave the faith are publicly Forsaken: stripped, declared spiritually dead before the congregation, and branded on the wrist with a glowing white circle by the Son's own light. The Chosen may never again speak to them or acknowledge them; families shield their children from a Forsaken father, and a crowd may finish what the ritual began. Those who survive gather in an underground network sheltering in a ruined temple of the Wild Gods, checking every newcomer for the mark, under the pragmatic, survival-focused leadership of Meridia. They keep the old faith—"praise the Wild Gods"—while developing new practices shaped by persecution, and they strike back: their operatives Dominic and Jay set the blast that tore open Rivenglade's Zenithian Hall. A humiliating path of penance exists for those who would return, but the readmitted are rebranded with a white feather and never fully restored.
Composition: Zenithian exiles, refugees, dissidents
Leadership: Meridia; scouts like Wren
Methods: Hidden communities, guerrilla resistance
The Forsaken Mark Hidden Sanctuaries Resistance Operations Wild God Preservation

Scholarly Institutions

Knowledge Preservation Networks
Organizations dedicated to the study and preservation of knowledge, including truths lost beneath conquest and doctrine. Chief among them is the Hollow Spire Scholarly Consortium—"a sanctuary for learning where academic pursuit is not bound by institutional prejudice, political pressure, or economic privilege"—backed by the Continental Council of Scholars and operating under the Vigilant Eye of the Archive Eternal, which offers chartered fellowships, archives, and protection to researchers of every background who pursue controversial or suppressed knowledge. Tanneus G'Raja compiled The Lost Histories as a Scholar of the Hollow Spire; the amateur scholar Alaquinn Althaea sought its fellowship for her study of the nature spirits.
Methods: Crumbling sanctuaries, forbidden texts, the dreams of the dying
Philosophy: "Truth, however painful, deserves witness"
Resources: The Hollow Spire Scholarly Consortium; chartered fellowships
Chartered Fellowships The Archive Eternal Bearing Witness

Military & Political Orders

Where blade and bureaucracy intersect, order emerges from chaos through the disciplined application of authority, whether righteous or tyrannical.

The Reclamation Zone

Military Government of the Age of Gods
The military government of the Reclamation Zone in the Age of Gods—war-torn territories of ashen forests, gouged farmlands, and collapsed bridges, held under the authority of Governor Verin, a sharp-eyed woman risen through unflinching pragmatism and ruthless discipline. From a requisitioned manor house, with clerks at her side, she demanded documented identity and passage papers of strangers, dispatched agents to observe them, and convened a religious council of recognized consistories to examine them for heretical belief; an undocumented healer who fed the hungry could be named a heresiarch and expelled beyond the perimeter of settled territory. She saw herself as a bulwark against hidden contagions—ideologies, creeds, mythologies—that might unravel her hard-won post-war order.
Structure: Governor Verin; her clerks, agents, and soldiers
Control Methods: Passage papers, agents, religious council
Territory: The Reclamation Zone (Age of Gods)
Military Rule Passage Papers Religious Council

The Chroniclers of Bone

Militant Scholarly Order
Scholars and mystics based in the Hollow Spire, a fortress-city fashioned from the skeleton of a colossal Titan, dedicated to uncovering the mysteries of the gods—and, in some hands, to seeking ways to sever humanity's ties to godhood. Their deepest archives hold forbidden texts that hinted at a god yet to awaken. In the years before the Silence, High Inquisitor Mordain Ashcroft made the Spire a sentinel of rigid faith, hunting the Bloodmarked as abominations with his Eyes of the Spire—seven devoted spies sent across the realms—until ancient texts revealed that the Bloodmarked were champions against the void, and his crusade had served the very entity he sought to oppose.
Base: The Hollow Spire (Titan bone city)
Mission: Supernatural threat elimination
Resources: Vast libraries, forbidden texts
Divine Wars Knowledge Supernatural Combat Bone Architecture

Tribal & Cultural Societies

From the strength-ruled wastes to the harmony-seeking forests, ancient peoples adapt their wisdom to a world forever changed by divine conflict.

The Ashborn Tribes

Post-Apocalyptic Nomads
Nomadic scavenger tribes of the Godscarred Wastes who worship the remnants of the fallen gods and search the ancient battlefields for fragments of divine relics, prized for power more than truth and traded like coin. Born breathing poison and aging badly for it, they drink fermented mare's milk—the traditional drink of truth-telling—sleep in beds that sway with the wind, and read the whisper-language of the Wastes: the wind's sigh across petrified bone, the tremor that heralds a Stonehide Titan. They teach their children to fear the Bloodmarked as a curse and to check for signs of the mark. Kaelen R'Veyne, the Silver Hunter, was born among them; so was the historian Tanneus G'Raja, who chose ink over plunder. What their war-songs remember, and what they have forgotten, is a question for The Divine War.
Origin: The Godscarred Wastes (see The Divine War)
Territory: Godscarred Wastes
Specialization: Corruption detection, relic hunting
Heritage: Relic-hunters; fear of the mark
Relic Hunters Nomadic Lifestyle Fear of the Mark Wasteland Adaptation

The Bloodfang Clans

Beastmen Warrior Societies
Nomadic berserker tribes of the Crimson Tundra, following the migrations of tundra beasts in a cycle of hunting, raiding, and survival, their society rigidly hierarchical and ruled by strength and savagery. They are an alliance of Beastmen species—hulking Ursine warriors, swift Lupine scouts and raiders, winged Avian sentinels, and lithe Feline hunters and assassins—each keeping its own tribe under Bloodfang law and their common veneration of the War God, a primal deity who demands strength, sacrifice, and ferocity. Central to their faith is Bloodmarked sacrifice: any child born with unusually potent Bloodmarked power is sacred, offered on frozen altars beneath the Blood Moon and its lifeblood consumed by the clan's warriors to amplify their strength. Ryx Shadowclaw fled that altar; his mother Lyra took his place.
Territory: Crimson Tundra
Composition: Multiple Beastmen species
Structure: Strength-based pack hierarchies
Worship: War God, primal violence
Blood Moon Ceremonies Nomadic Adaptation

Dawnseeker Society

Elven Forest Guardians
Reclusive elven guardians and stewards of the Verdant Veil, serving as protectors of the ancient forest realm where nature reigns supreme. Their entire civilization is built around preserving the delicate balance between nature and magic; they hear the whispers of the Wild Gods in leaf and stream and possess powerful healing and nature-controlling abilities. A royal house—Princess Elowen Faerwyn among its daughters—carries Dawnseeker diplomacy to the human kingdoms, and they live in hidden villages and treetop havens woven among the Veil's colossal trees. Sommer Isle's elves scorn them as "low elves" for their nature magic and Wild God reverence.
Territory: The Verdant Veil
Governance: Royal house
Magic: Forest consciousness communion
Nature-Magic Balance Forest Consciousness Post-Silence Recovery

Village Social Structures

Villages and Municipal Authorities
Small settlements like Oakhaven grow up around a crossroads and an inn, wary of strangers and quick to make legends of what lives in their woods. Larger towns grow their own bureaucracies: at Elden Ridge a Municipal Authority of magistrate, scribe, and warden captain issues cease-and-desist notices under Municipal Statute 847-B against "Unauthorized Investigation of Municipal Resources by Non-Credentialed Individuals," while its wardens protect the sacred animals bearing divine markings, and the village well is kept clean by a water sprite paid in bread and honey cakes.
Elden Ridge: Magistrate, scribe, warden captain
Wardens: Protectors of divinely marked animals
Statute: Municipal Statute 847-B
Municipal Authority Sacred Animal Wardens Water Sprite

Divine & Ancient Orders

Echoes of celestial governance and primordial protection systems, where the memory of divine intervention lingers in institutional form.

The Guardian Angels

Divine Protective Hierarchy
The protectors of mortals, commanded by Azrael, God of Guardian Angels—a hierarchy that stood between the divine and mortal realms and, as the old understanding went, did not walk the earth itself. Azrael held that mortals should not have to live in fear of the divine; what passed between him and his own kin cost him his place among the divine and left the mortal world without its protector. The story is told in The Sin of Mercy.
Commander: Azrael (historical)
Function: Protection of mortals from the divine
Status: Historical (pre-Withdrawal)
Mortal Protection Azrael Historical System

The Free People

Nomadic Tribes of the Age of Gods
Nomadic tribes of the Age of Gods living outside established authority—"We bow to no distant rulers," said their elder Marra, "nor do we accept their hollow authority over these lands"—who kept the older truths their woods remember. "We are not 'tribes,'" Marra told the soldiers of the Reclamation Zone; "we are the Free People. We bow to no governor's edicts." Around their fires they share simple meals of roasted game, wild roots and berries, and burn the seeing smoke—the vision-smoke ritual that reveals "not who you claim to be, but who you are beneath the naming. Beneath the forgetting." Their name is an old one, remembered from the days before mortals feared the divine, and it would be spoken again in the days of the Divine War.
Governance: Elders; no distant rulers
Legacy: Keepers of older truths
Elder: Marra (see The God Who Wept)
Sacred Site: The Lightning Oak shrine
Vision-Smoke Older Truths The Divine War

Organizational Dynamics & Relationships

Religious Tensions

Zenith's Chosen aggressively challenges the old temples and persecutes Wild God worshippers, while the Forsaken Communities emerge as direct resistance to Zenithian expansion and control.

Knowledge Conflicts

Scholarly Institutions operate in tension with the Chroniclers of Bone's militant approach and with the suppression of forbidden knowledge.

Post-Crisis Adaptation

The Silent God crisis has accelerated religious conversion, refugee displacement, and the formation of new alliances between previously isolated communities facing shared supernatural threats.
