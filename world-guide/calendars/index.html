Still Kept In
Bae'Ravah reckoning

The Arcane Calendar had marked time since Atonia first carved it from pure logic, and for the first years of the Great Withdrawal it went on marking it: priests kept their temples, believers kept their vigils, and the eight-day weeks turned as they always had. But as the silence lengthened, its divine mathematics began to feel like a mockery, counting days for gods who no longer counted mortals worthy of their presence. The merchants moved first. A 384-day year with perfect divisibility was elegant for tracking magical cycles and impractical for trade; the solar seasons did not align with thirty-two-day months, and an eight-day week meant market days fell irregularly. The Arcane reckoning did not vanish. The Bae'Ravah calendar of the Fus'Ravahn follows it still, and that tongue's words for day, week, month and year, with its names for the eight days and twelve months, are given on the Languages page under the Calendar tab.

Merchants and daily life

Athea planned twelve months of thirty days, three hundred and sixty in all, divisible and trackable, fit for commerce and comprehension alike. The Broken Chain would not be held to thirty. It took thirty-five and dragged the year out to meet the sun: 365 days, eleven reigns of thirty and one of thirty-five, and in some years a day that is not always there. The almanac numbers that leap day 59.5 and sets it inside the Lovers' reign, in the Dark Moon, between Day 59 and Day 60, "where it belongs and does not quite fit"; those born on it carry three readings rather than one. The five extra days of the Broken Chain stand outside the normal turning of the houses, extending the Void Temple, while the moon keeps her cycle and rolls from Day 30 back to Day 1. In the almanac each month is called by its constellation's reign, so that readers from another sky can find their way; but the Arcadian names are there beneath, and Arcadia keeps them.

The Godscarred Wastes ascendant

Every soul carries three marks written in starlight. The constellation of the birth date tells who you are; the moon phase of that date tells how you feel underneath; the rising sign, drawn from the constellation and the hour of birth, tells how you arrive. The house comes from the day of the month within the constellation's reign and says which doorway all three came through. Arcadia's regions each hold a sixth of the day, four hours apiece, and the hour of your birth does not tell where you were born but which country's heaven was awake. A birth that lands exactly on one of the six turnings, midnight, 04:00, 08:00, noon, 16:00 or 20:00, belongs to both skies that touch it. When the stars became history

Five constellations have manifested in the story timeline; seven remain prophesied. This follows events within the world, not publication order. A constellation can already exist even when the book telling its origin is still in progress.

Athea’s Almanac belongs to the Great Withdrawal, when four constellations had manifested. The Awakener follows later, in The Silence. The oracle books stay in their own time; this guide can follow later developments. These entries reveal story events.

The Constellations of Prophecy

As Athea recorded them thirty years into the Great Withdrawal: of the twelve constellations that name the months, four burned in the sky and eight waited in the void, prophecies hungering to become history. One of the eight has since begun to burn; readers of The Silence will know which.

Read all twelve constellation histories & prophecies
The Awakener
Month 1 - Primaven · Transformation
Unborn when Athea wrote, but stirring, "not yet born but wishing to exist." Her prophecy: "The Awakener shall blaze when silence itself learns to speak. Words shall break from the void to find their vessels, and in their hearing, the unmaking begins." Those born beneath it, she foresaw, would hear the silence between breaths, feel tremors before earthquakes, and know when sleeping things begin to dream again. What stirred, and what it woke, is the matter of The Silence.
Foretold by Athea · Kindled in The Silence
The Lovers
Month 2 - Loriven · Eternal Return
Two lights circling endlessly above us, never truly meeting, one remembering and one forgetting. The Lovers bear eternal testimony to the cursed devotion of Elodias and Senia, bound by the jealous weaving of Malritha the Weaver: he finds her in every incarnation, loves her for seven days, and loses her again. Athea says the constellation formed in the instant of a choice Elodias made between endless loss and never seeing her at all; the choice, and its cost, are the subject of Seven Days of Sun and The Weaver of Sorrows.
Currently Visible
The Garden
Month 3 - Verdanis · Paradise
Will manifest when paradise is either found or forever lost. The prophecy promises: "In the Garden's light, mortals shall either reclaim their first innocence or watch it wither beyond all recovery." Athea sees paradise and poison growing from the same soil, a transformation that might be salvation or damnation depending on who tends the garden and what they are willing to sacrifice for their vision of perfection.
Not Yet Formed
The Crown
Month 4 - Solmere · Sovereignty
Foretold to appear when true sovereignty is claimed. "The Crown shall manifest when the true sovereign awakens to their burden. Not born to power but chosen by necessity." Athea sees reluctant authority: leadership that arrives not through ambition but through the terrible realisation that if you do not lead, everyone dies.
Not Yet Formed
The Guardians
Month 5 - Umbralux · Protection
Will emerge when protectors arise in the hour of greatest need. Athea wept as she spoke this prophecy: "The Guardians shall stand between light and shadow, and their formation shall mean that something precious enough to die for has finally been found—or is about to be lost."
Not Yet Formed
The Wanderer
Month 6 - Wanderis · Endless Journey
The constellation holds Thainos's thousand-year journey in its pattern: the god who shaped humanity from clay and walked every road seeking one soul untouched by cruelty, helping every soul he met, finding beauty everywhere and perfection nowhere. Athea says it blazed into being not to mark an ending but a multiplication, the moment he understood that the search itself had been the answer, that by seeking goodness he had planted it everywhere he walked, his essence scattered like seeds across all humanity. How the search ended is told in The God Who Wept.
Currently Visible
The Shepherd
Month 7 - Goldmere · Guidance
Will manifest when the lost flock is gathered or scattered forever. The prophecy warns: "The Shepherd's crook shall appear in stars when one must choose between saving the many or preserving the few." Not all who are led shall wish to follow.
Not Yet Formed
The Hunter
Month 8 - Thornmarch · The Chase
Born from tragedy, and formed in the moment of ultimate corruption. It began with Astrid, whose friend Natalia died because they lingered five more minutes to watch a sunset, and who became, in her grief and guilt, the first Bloodmarked. Celebrated as heroes and protectors, thrown flowers in every square, the Bloodmarked saved the world by losing their souls: during the Divine War, as gods fell from heaven like asteroids and comets, they descended into a battle-frenzy so complete they could no longer tell friend from foe. The constellation crystallised in that moment of realisation, heroes become horrors in their victory. How Astrid came by a god's blood is told in The Divine War.
Currently Visible
The Storm
Month 9 - Stormend · Necessary Destruction
Prophesied to form when chaos becomes necessary for renewal. "The Storm shall rage in heaven when the old order must be swept away. Its winds shall carry both destruction and the seeds of what comes after." Some shelters will stand; most will fall.
Not Yet Formed
The Tower
Month 10 - Frostmere · Isolation
Will appear when isolation becomes either sanctuary or prison. "The Tower rises in stars when one must stand alone against many, or when many unite against one. Its height shall measure the distance between earth and heaven—or the depth of the fall."
Not Yet Formed
The Dragon
Month 11 - Drakonis · Forbidden Knowledge
The most ancient constellation, older than memory, older than the gods themselves. It appeared when the First Gods discovered something that predated them, something so terrible they built it a prison among the stars. What lies imprisoned, none can say, for those who knew took that knowledge to obliteration rather than risk its escape. Since its formation, all who grieve have heard the same song in their sorrow; every culture names it differently, never questioning why the melody never changes. Within its notes, some claim to hear promises of paradise unlocked, of death undone, of reunion with all that was lost. But the wise wonder: would such a paradise be heaven brought to earth, or heaven consuming it? The First Gods were not fools. What they hid, they hid for reason.
Currently Visible
The Broken Chain
Month 12 - Veilmorn · Liberation
The final constellation, and the one that terrifies Athea most, for when it forms something that has held reality together since the first moment will shatter. "The Broken Chain shall scatter across the sky when the oldest bond finally breaks. Freedom and catastrophe shall be one word spoken in different tongues." Liberation or annihilation: the stars do not say which, perhaps because they are the same event seen from different angles. Its reign alone refuses the thirty-day rule and claims thirty-five.
Not Yet Formed

Athea the Weaver of Stars

Read Athea’s story and the origins of the Almanac

Athea was born beneath a new moon with white-over eyes that would never open to ordinary light. The midwives called it a curse and the priests called it judgment; her mother called it unbearable. On the night of the child's first full moon she carried her to a tidal cave where divine presence still lingered, one of the thin places where the infinite had worn reality smooth, wrote her plea with tears as ink, and laid the baby in a pool where moonlight gathered like milk, offering her years, her joy, her very soul if necessary.

Something answered. Athea has never learned which god touched her head that night, and has come to think that the shadow hiding it is not deep but far: whatever answered did not rise out of Arcadian ground but came in on the tide, and left in the back of her throat the taste of an ocean she has never sailed. When her mother lifted her from the pool her hair had turned white as moonlight, and though her eyes stayed sealed to ordinary light, they had opened to star essence, the light between light. The sirens, she suspects, knew. They have never once been surprised by her.

Through siren bone needles, gifts from depths no living thing should visit, she reads the night sky and speaks of future events in the present tense. She sees all three states of time at once: what was, what is, and what hungers to become. In her thirtieth year a delegation of merchants, scholars and desperate souls came to her asking for a new calendar, something practical for trade and something to fill the silence the gods had left, and she said yes.

"Twelve stories. Twelve turnings. Four written in light, eight await their time."

Twelve songs sang back to her through the bone needles: four constellations already burning, eight that existed only as potential, stars that will form when certain events come to pass and certain choices are made. She named the months for what she heard, then drew the rest of the system after them: the seventy-eight Blood and Bone cards, the moon's secret calendar of eight phases, the twelve houses, and the rising signs. Her daily horoscopes, she insists, are not commands from heaven but weather reports for the soul. The Astronomer's Calendar thus became not just a measure of time but a map of prophecy written across the heavens, and her almanac calls itself the gentle lie of stellar guidance, "not truth, but what you can bear." Her testament, the twelve constellations, and a reading for every day of the year are collected in The Siren Bone Oracle: Almanac of Stars.
