Region · Island Nation · Southwest of Arcadia

Nareen

Nah-REEN — "Land of Light and Flowers," from the old words nar (sunlight, radiance) and een (living earth). A crescent of eternal spring ringed by warm seas and constant mist, where every stone remembers, every wave carries a prayer, and every blossom that falls is a wish ascending.

Setting of The Girl Beneath the Cherry Tree — a companion novel in progress

The Island

A large, crescent-shaped island southwest of Arcadia, Nareen is a land where warmth never truly fades. Its terrain climbs from sun-washed coasts to mist-laden highlands, every slope alive with terraces of green and bloom, and the air always carries the sound of water and wind — streams weaving through valleys, chimes ringing from cliffside homes, the distant rush of unseen waterfalls.

The Blossom Cliffs

Sacred formation · Coast

Pale cliffs of sun-bleached stone lined with rows of cherry trees whose petals drift down into the sea. Their roots grow into the rock itself, fed by the mist that rises perpetually from the water below.

Among the most sacred places in Nareen: here the boundary between land and sea — and so between the mortal world and the spirit realm — is at its thinnest.

The Vale of Steps

Sacred valley · Domain of Oruen

A hidden valley of slow-moving creeks and stepping stones where locals say the gods come to rest. Offerings of petals and woven reeds are left on the stones each morning.

Oruen, the Dreaming Mountain, is said to slumber beneath it. Whisper a prayer across the stepping stones and the mist shifts in reply — faces, symbols, faint lights in the fog. Oruen remembers every prayer ever spoken there.

The High Terraces

Agriculture · Highlands

Plateaus carved by hand over centuries, filled with fruit trees, rice fields, and flower gardens — from a distance a mosaic of greens and pinks against white stone.

The terraces embody the Nareenese philosophy of patient, generational labor: shaping the land not through force but through continuance.

The Wind Markets

Towns · Harbors

Seaside towns built into hillsides, homes carved from pale rock, bells and shells hanging from every balcony. When the wind passes through, an entire village hums like a single instrument.

These are where daily life unfolds — markets, harbors, gathering places where the rhythm of the sea sets the rhythm of everything.

Night in Nareen

The quiet hours

At night mist rises from the valleys and veils the land in silver, and the chimes grow faint. The Nareenese say this is when the spirits move between dreams and waking — the boundary between worlds dissolving in the hours before dawn.

The Nareenese

Warm skin, sun-bleached hair, and golden eyes that catch the light like water. Their movements are unhurried, their speech melodic. They are not a warlike people; they build, tend, sing, and remember. They see beauty not in perfection but in continuance — the ability of life to go on blooming, even after loss.

Appearance

Tawny, bronze, or honey-toned skin; hair dark brown to near-black, often lightened by the sun into streaks of gold or amber; eyes of brown, gold, or amber, reflecting river light. Draped fabrics in muted tones — pale coral, ochre, white, sea green — with lightweight shawls and embroidered sashes. Homes smell of salt air, sandalwood, and flower water.

Temperament

Quiet humor, patient temperaments, deep emotional loyalty. Words are believed to carry resonance beyond speech, so conversation is careful, deliberate, often poetic.

Love songs and lullabies are common but sung in private, meant to "anchor" the soul of a loved one — music, they believe, binds the spirit to the body and keeps it from wandering.

The White-Haired Blessed

Those whose hair turns white are recognized as blessed by Maiku, the sea god. They can understand the sirens' language, receive gifts from them, and serve as bridges between the two peoples.

Faith: The Four Who Rest

Nareenese spirituality is fluid, centered on natural divinity rather than doctrine. Each part of the land houses a resting spirit, and to disturb balance is to wake sorrow. The Four Benevolent Spirits are said to sleep lightly, hearing every whispered prayer that touches water or air.

Sireh — The River Rest

Flow · Dreams · Forgiveness

A water nymph who traveled the world gathering the most beautiful dreams to offer the gods, so they might one day be made real. Weary after centuries, she found peace in Nareen's gentle currents.

Petals are left in creeks in hope that dreams come true. Those plagued by nightmares drink from a slow creek out of cupped palms to wash the bad dreams away.

Leonas — The Sun Weaver

Warmth · Love · Vitality

Born one of the Bintu — creatures of pure light made from the Sun's essence by the Old Gods, before even the First Gods — beings split in two at birth and fated to seek their other half. The Bintu are the original soul mates. Leonas's was Mela, who died of a broken heart; his spirit now watches over the land they made their home, blessing lovers.

Couples hang seashell chimes between trees to "catch" his blessing; newborns are swaddled with a sun chip, and Sun Lilies are set on the windowsill on a child's first birthday in remembrance of Mela.

Tao — The Stone Sleeper

Endurance · Patience

Tao walked the whole island in life; in spirit he helps Oruen guide people to where they are meant to be and find rest for their souls. Pilgrims leave smooth river stones at shrines as promises to keep faith.

Mairu — The Breath of Rain

Renewal · Change

A siren who became human; her spirit lingers on land with her chosen people. Farmers whisper prayers before the morning mist lifts, to "wake" her gently.

The Greater Deities

Chiru — The Voice in the Wind

Goddess of the Wind · Guardian of Lighthouses

A village woman who married a fisherman and waited faithfully for his return; when a storm took his ship, she waited forever. Now seafarers' families pray to her for safe return and fair weather. Her signs: a chime ringing with no wind, sudden warmth after rain, sunlight moving against the tide.

Oruen — The Dreaming Mountain

The Mist Father · Patience · Hidden wisdom

A god so taken with mortals he wished to become one. He wanders the mist in mortal form, wearing a new face each time, guiding lost souls. He slumbers beneath the Vale of Steps. His signs: mist that lingers after sunrise, a dry stone path that should be wet, a soft voice calling your name when no one is near.

Luralei — The Keeper of Gentle Renewal

The Blossom Goddess · Growth · Healing · Luck

A young goddess with long flowing pink hair, sometimes mischievous — granting wishes in unexpected ways to teach lessons. Wishes are whispered beneath blossoms; each falling petal is a chance. Her signs: out-of-season blooms, the scent of flowers in strange places, a little girl's laugh in the breeze.

Maiku — The Keeper of Currents

The Sea God · Distance · Longing · Connection

Creator of the sirens. Maiku carries messages, prayers, and memories across oceans: a letter sealed in a bottle or folded in bark paper and cast to the waves is read by Maiku, who decides whether to carry it to its soul — living or beyond. His symbol is a spiral shell with a single hole, used to "hear" the sea.

The Sacred Pact with the Sirens

Nareenese and sirens live closely along the coasts, bound by a covenant of mutual respect. After storms the people leave offerings of fish to honor Maiku's creation; in return the sirens let their magical artifacts wash ashore for the people to use. If a dead siren washes up, it must be buried on the beach beneath a tomb of seashells so its soul can return to the sea.

The Four Festivals

Spring Rite of Blossoms

Honoring Luralei

For every birth of the previous year, a cherry tree is planted. Each spring, people carve a symbol, name, or phrase into the base of a cherry tree if they wish it to come true — a carving that becomes part of the bark forever and cannot be undone. Cutting a healthy cherry tree is forbidden; carved bark from a felled tree must be burned separately to release its blessings.

Summer Wind Festival

Honoring Chiru

Thousands of chimes are hung from terraces and doorways so whole villages sing with a thousand voices — a chorus said to carry the ancestors home. Those who lost someone to the sea walk to the lighthouse at dusk on a windy night; sometimes the wind returns their loved one's name.

Autumn Festival of Mist

Honoring Oruen

When the trees turn copper, Oruen walks the land more often in mortal guise. On autumn full moons the people make the Silent Pilgrimage to the Vale of Steps, giving written prayers on bark paper to the streams. To speak is a grave disrespect: the silence is a form of listening.

Winter Sea Festival

Honoring Maiku

A festival of exchange and covenant. Food is left on the ocean rocks for the sirens, who carry it to the deep where the spirit land meets the mortal world; in return they leave water from the deepest trenches, black sand from the ocean floor, and shells found only in the depths — sacred materials for magic and worship.

Naru, the Old Tongue

Most Nareenese now speak the common tongue, but the oldest still speak Naru — flowing, water-like, and highly compositional: complex meanings are built by joining simpler roots, reflecting a worldview in which all things are connected. Seru (large) + mai (water) = serumai, ocean. Wama (flow) + mai = wamamai, river. Verbs are not conjugated for tense; sei serves as "to be" for every person: Matsu sei monea — love is sacred.
