Northern Wilderlands

"Where ancient powers walk openly and time itself bends to primordial will"

Vast untamed regions where human settlement remains sparse and the very foundations of reality grow thin. These lands serve as refuge for those fleeing civilization's complications and testing ground for those seeking to prove themselves against primordial forces. Here, mountain peaks pierce the boundary between realms, forests remember every song ever sung, and deserts reflect skies from other times and places.

Realm of Ancient Powers

The Northern Wilderlands are vast regions where human settlement remains sparse and ancient powers still walk openly. Time flows strangely in these regions, and avalanches can bury travelers in different centuries.

These lands serve as refuge for those fleeing civilization's complications—sanctuary for outcasts, exiles, and those who seek to escape the growing control of organized powers—and as testing ground for those seeking to prove themselves against primordial forces.

Much of what is recorded of these lands comes from the compendium compiled from the chronicles of the scholar Tanneus G'Raja, Keeper of Divine Sorrows, who set down what he believed beyond what he could prove.

The Howling Peaks

Mountains that rise so high their summits pierce the boundary between material and divine realms, where mountain stone touches celestial wind and time flows strangely. Avalanches here don't just bury travelers—they can bury them in different centuries, displacing the unwary across decades or even ages.

The Singing Forests

Ancient woodlands where trees remember every song ever sung beneath their boughs, serving as unwitting archives of all music ever created within their bounds. Travelers report hearing melodies from lost civilizations, battle hymns from ancient wars, and lullabies sung by mothers whose children died before learning the words.

Musical Memory Living Archives Ancient Melodies Sound Preservation

The Glass Desert

A crystalline wasteland formed when celestial fire struck ancient sands during the Divine Wars. The desert's surface reflects not the present sky but skies from other times and places. Navigation requires special techniques known only to the Sand Readers, nomadic guides who can interpret which reflections show reality and which show dreams.
