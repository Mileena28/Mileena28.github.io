Solara

From Divine Fire to Ashes, From Ashes to Hope

Once the Golden City, the jewel of the South, Solara now stands as monument to both celestial glory and cosmic tragedy. Raised upon the fertile plains where the Sun God Solarius is believed to have fallen in the ancient celestial war, and lit by a sun that never seemed to set, this kingdom of polished white stone and shimmering gold was ruled by Bloodmarked kings who wielded controlled divine fire. In the age of the Silence it was reduced to charred ruins by its own second prince, Dain Solarius, possessed by the Silent God—yet even from its ashes, returning survivors have tried to sow the seeds of renewal.

The Divine Metamorphosis

From the trading kingdom of Moradim to the golden paradise of Solara—the same land under two names

Ancient Moradim

The Kingdom Before the Divine War

Before the Divine War, the land that is now Solara was the Kingdom of Moradim, a wealthy mainland realm of trade routes and mainland connections, its royal house securing alliances through diplomatic marriage—it was Moradim's wealth that the island kingdom of Fayespire needed to save its people from starvation, and Moradim's eastern provinces that looked to the match for their financial salvation.

It was in this Moradim, in the age the Lost Histories remember, that Princess Jade dreamed of her own city grown strange with centuries—its buildings reaching higher, its streets laid in patterns not yet conceived, and every one of them emptied of life beneath the gaze of a prince with void-black eyes. Readers of The Silence will recognise what she saw.

Solara Rising

Born from Divine Essence

After the Divine War, Moradim was Moradim no longer: the kingdom that rose in its place bore the name Solara. How the trading kingdom of old became a sun-drenched paradise where golden light emanated from the very stones is a matter for The Divine War; The Silence records only that Solara was raised upon the fertile plains where the Sun God Solarius is believed to have fallen in the ancient celestial war.

Its capital became a city of polished white stone and shimmering gold, its graceful arches and soaring spires reaching for a perpetual sun, while gardens overflowed with fire lilies and sun orchids that bloomed year-round. Mountains rose in layered purple and gold to the west, the ocean glittered like a sliver of sapphire beyond, and the weather, its people swore, was always perfect. Solara grew into a centre of civilisation, art and martial prowess: bustling markets, sacred academies where warriors trained in combat and strategy, gladiatorial arenas that echoed with cheers, temples that resonated with fervent prayer to the Sun God, mines that left the kingdom overflowing with jewels. Its people were renowned for their artistry, their passion and their faith in Solarius’s legacy.

Power rested with an elite of Bloodmarked nobles who wielded controlled aspects of divine fire, and at its heart lay the Crown of Aetheris, the kingdom’s most sacred relic—forged in the fires of a dying star and said to hold the last spark of Solarius’s divine consciousness. Only Solara’s king or queen could wear it, and only in sacred ceremony or national crisis; worn, it amplified the wearer’s Bloodmarked fire many times over and whispered the Sun God’s guidance directly into the mind. King Theron Solarius, wise and just, wore it with solemn gravity through a long reign of peace; his elder son Valerius, thoughtful and universally loved, was his undisputed heir.

The Beloved Prince

From the Phoenix Blade's golden glory to a corruption no one in Solara saw coming

Dain the Extravagant

Before his corruption, Prince Dain Solarius was the brightest jewel of Solara’s radiant court—a prince sculpted from gold and imbued with flame, with sun-kissed skin, golden-blonde hair and amber eyes that glowed with the divine fire of his Bloodmarked lineage. He embodied every virtue his culture celebrated: honour, glory, passion, loyalty. The populace adored him, the nobility respected him, and his soldiers genuinely loved him. Solarians remembered him as a man with a kind word and a smile for everyone, open-handed to the point of extravagance—the prince who once sent a lady of the court an entire garden of star-blossoms for her star day. Those who survived the fall still call him “Dain the Extravagant.”

As the “Phoenix Blade,” he was the unmatched darling of Solara’s gladiatorial arena, wielding a glaive wreathed in living flame and propelling himself through the air on bursts of fire, a warrior who fought with grace and honour. Yet he was the second son, forever in the shadow of his elder brother Valerius, and it was into that quiet ache that the Silent God whispered a single word: Burn. His unrequited pursuit of Princess Elowen of the Dawnseekers, his jealousy of the Silver Hunter, and his hunger to be more than “Valerius’s charming younger brother” were twisted into a creed of purification by fire. This made his transformation all the more tragic and, to his people, inexplicable—the destruction of everything golden about Solara came at the hands of the one who had embodied its greatest virtues.

The Night Everything Changed

How the Shining City became a monument to loss

The Speed of Collapse

The fall of Solara occurred with shocking rapidity during the Silence. It began in the gladiatorial arena, where Dain, before a mesmerised crowd, beat a defeated gladiator to death with his bare hands and then preached that “only absolute fire, absolute will, and absolute silence will crown us in the age to come,” casting the elves, the Spire and Atheria as scheming enemies at Solara’s gates. Valerius fled the royal box while the crowd chanted his brother’s name and, twined with it, a single word: Silence. One day it was the Shining City, the jewel of the South, and almost overnight it became chaos. People fled leaving everything behind—doors ripped from their hinges, shops gaping like black voids, children’s toys scattered in the ash by hands that never returned. The transformation caught everyone off-guard, including Valerius, who had gravely underestimated whatever was taking hold of his brother.

The King's Flight

In the Grand Hall, Valerius made his last stand with the dwindling ranks of Solara’s loyalists, raising the ancestral blade of his house against a brother who no longer answered to his name. Dain’s corrupted black fire ate through his golden armour and threaded darkness into his flesh; outmatched and broken, Valerius turned and fled with the last of his legion into the twilight, ghosts slipping away from a city conquered by shadow. Then the Silent God, wearing Dain’s skin, seated himself on the golden dais and lifted the Crown of Aetheris onto his own head. What became of Valerius afterward—and how the soldiers who followed him remember it—is a bitter thread taken up in The Forsaken.

Monument to Devastation

The once-golden city became a charred skeleton of its former glory, buildings standing like broken teeth against the fading light. Golden facades were blackened and scarred, the streets lay coated in a thick grey ash that muffled every footstep, and the air hung heavy with smoke, decay and a cloying, sickening sweetness. A pall of black smoke stained the sky a bruised purple above the ruins, while void-corrupted Beastborn patrolled the broken walls like monstrous sentinels—a monument to how quickly divine glory could turn to cosmic horror.

Seeds of Renewal

When the Silence lifted, Solara, though forever marked, was not abandoned: seeds of resilience were sown amid the ruins, and the golden spirit of the city flickered in the determined faces of returning people who meant to build it anew, brick by painstaking brick. But the scars ran deep. Void-corrupted Beastborn hungered at the edges of the ruins, the shattered Crown lay scattered across the Grand Hall, and in the years that followed most of Solara’s people gave up the fight—they became the soot-stained refugees who washed up on the piers of Rivenglade and elsewhere, mourning a city that, in the words of one loyal soldier, is “all gone now.”

The Untouched Sanctuary

Where divine grace preserved beauty amid universal devastation

Temple of Solara

A colossal cathedral of white marble that remained impossibly untouched by the fires that ravaged the city, its spire piercing the smoke-laden sky like a defiant finger pointed at the heavens. Gold filigree traces its soaring arches, gold leaf gleams on carvings of celestial bodies and floral motifs, and sunlight streams through stained-glass windows depicting scenes of Solarian deities, casting rainbows across polished marble floors.

Divine Protection

The temple maintains an inexplicable sanctuary effect that repels Beastborn and Void Wraiths, creating a safe haven in the midst of corruption. This protection suggests that some divine forces remain active even when others have withdrawn or fallen.

Sacred Nexus

The temple serves as a nexus point where the veil between worlds grows thin, porous and receptive, making it ideal for the most profound magical workings. Before the main altar stands a massive pedestal of gleaming white granite inlaid with crystals that pulse with an inner light—the nexus itself, radiating an ancient, potent energy even amid the cosmic chaos surrounding it.

Preserved Beauty

Despite the surrounding devastation, the temple maintains its pristine condition as if protected by divine grace. Its untouched magnificence serves as both sanctuary for survivors and reminder of Solara's former celestial glory.

Where Light Answered

In the last hours of the Silence, a ritual worked at the temple’s crystal pedestal called down an Angel of Light—a being of terrible beauty with void-black eyes, red pupils and a diamond sword—at the price of one man’s complete erasure from existence. The shockwave of pure, restorative light that erupted from the temple swept across the whole realm, pushing back the void and breaking the Silent God’s hold. Of the one who paid that price, only a bone staff remained on the marble floor.

Phoenix Rising from Ashes

Solara stands as testament to the cycle of destruction and renewal, where divine fire transforms tragedy into transcendence. From the blessed ground where a Sun God fell emerged a paradise of golden light, only to be consumed by a corruption born from within. Here, in ruins that gleam with remembered glory, the few who return work to restore not just buildings but the promise that light can emerge from any darkness—even as ghosts, scavengers and void-corrupted Beastborn keep most travellers from lingering long in the cursed city. This is Solara's hard truth: that every ending may contain the seed of a new beginning, but the seed must be tended by those willing to live among the ashes.

The Royal Gardens

Once overflowing with fire lilies and sun orchids that bloomed year-round, the royal gardens held a fountain in which a restless prince once studied his own reflection, and a sacred flame at their heart where Valerius sought his brother out as the change first took root in him. Now they are scorched earth, choked by weeds and ash—though survivors still hope to see fire lilies bloom there again.

The Grand Hall

The heart of Solarian power: a cavernous throne room of marble floors, opulent tapestries and proud banners, its golden dais crowned by the ancient, sacred seat of the kings. Here Valerius fell before his brother's black fire, here the Silent God took up the Crown of Aetheris and tore open reality to summon his Void Wraiths, and here the Silence was finally ended. The shattered Crown lies scattered amid the ruins.

The Arena

Solara's gladiatorial arena, where banners emblazoned with flaming sigils snapped above a sea of chanting faces and the royal box looked down on the sand. Prince Dain's private chamber beneath its stands dripped with gold chased into flame patterns; it was on this sand that the Phoenix Blade's honour died and Solara first turned toward the void.

The Luminary's Quill

A shuttered bookshop in the ash-choked streets, its half-torn sign still creaking above the door. In the ruined city it became a fragile sanctuary for the four who came to end the Silence—a place to breathe, to plan, and to gather what strength the broken city still offered.
