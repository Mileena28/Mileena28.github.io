Hollow Spire

Fortress of Bone and Guardian of Forbidden Knowledge

Rising from the bleached remains of a fallen Titan, ringed by steep cliffs and misty valleys, this fortress-city stands as a monument to mortality and the legacy of the gods—a place of enlightenment and of isolation. Within its bone-white halls the Chroniclers of Bone study the legacies of the fallen gods and seek ways to sever humanity's ties to godhood, while in its deepest archives forbidden texts hint at a god yet to awaken.

From Death Comes Purpose

The transformation of ancient death into a living city dedicated to preserving cosmic order

The Fallen Titan

Ancient Colossus & Divine Battleground

In the cosmic upheaval of the Divine War, a primordial Titan fell to earth, its massive skeleton crashing across the landscape as divine conflicts raged overhead. This ancient being, vast beyond mortal comprehension, became both casualty and witness to the terrible price of divine anger; the world itself bears such scars, mountains formed from the ribs of titans and lakes that burn with celestial fire.

The Titan's bleached bones became, in time, the foundation of a fortress of knowledge in a world the gods had abandoned—a monument to mortality and the legacy of the gods.

The Bone Citadel

Fortress of Knowledge & Order

From this foundation of death arose a city like no other, its labyrinthine corridors and shadowed chambers hewn from the Titan's skeleton. The entire structure gleams bone-white amid the cliffs and mists that isolate it, its walls smooth and unnervingly light-absorbing, spiralling upward into shadowed heights and downward into bone-choked crypts. The air is cold, dry and dust-laden; the silence heavy and profound; bone torches and candlelight cast grotesque, dancing shadows across carvings of gaunt, skeletal figures.

Every surface bears the marks of careful construction: a labyrinth of libraries, chapels, studies and dungeons hewn from the very bones of the Titan. Its highest tower is a solitary chamber open to the wind; beneath it lie the grand chapel, the execution chamber, and the ossuary heart where the Spire's darkest counsels are held. The oppressive atmosphere of constant death weighs on inhabitants' minds, but also serves as reminder of the price of cosmic failure. Beyond the walls a dense, ancient forest presses close, and not far into those dark woods lies Oakhaven, long a stop for pilgrims and travellers bound for the Spire.

The Chroniclers of Bone

A fellowship founded to preserve knowledge, whose mission hardened, under one man's zeal, into inquisition

Guardians of Cosmic Order

The Spire began as a place of open inquiry. The Hollow Spire Scholarly Consortium—“a fellowship for the preservation and advancement of knowledge”—posted its invitations on municipal notice boards across the realms, seeking dedicated scholars of all backgrounds and experience levels, promising a sanctuary for learning unbound by institutional prejudice, political pressure or economic privilege, and protection for those pursuing controversial or suppressed knowledge. Applications were addressed to its Review Committee, care of the Morgyn Raven Route Postal Exchange, “Where Stone Meets Shadow.” The Ashborn historian Tanneus G'Raja, compiler of the Lost Histories, signs himself a Scholar of the Hollow Spire.

Its scholars and mystics, the Chroniclers of Bone, dedicated themselves to uncovering the mysteries of the gods and to seeking ways to sever humanity's ties to godhood. Within vast libraries carved into the Titan's bones they preserve dangerous knowledge—forbidden texts unearthed from the deepest archives that speak of the primordial balance between the Wild Gods and a god beyond the pantheon, of the Void Wraiths who serve him, and of the true origin of the Bloodmarked. Some Chroniclers approach this work with scholarly detachment, even open-minded inquiry. But by the age of the Silence the Spire's most unforgiving doctrine, enforced by High Inquisitor Mordain Ashcroft, held that the Bloodmarked were an abomination the Wild Gods demanded eradicated. Inquisitions were pronounced in its sombre halls, and its executioner's bone staff drew the life from the condemned—among them a young Dawnseeker healer whose only crime was the nature magic in her blood. The Spire became both protector and oppressor: a house of learning that had made itself a stronghold of dogma.

Shadows Within Order

Quiet dissent, a Groundkeeper's key and a hidden waterway beneath the Inquisitor's dungeons

The Hidden Resistance

Despite official doctrine, not every soul in the Spire shared the Inquisitor's certainty. Some Chroniclers harboured quiet doubts about his methods, though such voices were rarely raised and more rarely heeded, for to question him was to risk the charge of heresy. Deeper dissent hid beneath cowls: when Kaelen R'Veyne and Princess Elowen were dragged to the dungeons, it was a Beastman acolyte—a stray far from the Crimson Tundra like the cub who came to free them—who pressed the Groundkeeper's key, the master key to the dungeons, into Ryx Shadowclaw's paw and whispered directions to a hidden cellar door. “No war god honors such butchery,” he said of Mordain's sacrifices. The Inquisitor's fury at the “conspirators” and “traitors within my own Spire” shook the bone foundations, but he never found them.

Underground Passages

A secret waterway flows beneath the dungeon floor, reached through a hidden cellar door: a dark, waist-deep tunnel that runs out beyond the Spire's walls and empties into the sweet night air of the bordering forest. That route carried three prisoners to freedom, and to a waterfall cave in the woods where a broken Inquisitor would later come seeking atonement.

Life Among the Bones

The rhythm of existence within a city carved from death itself

Scholarly Pursuits

The libraries and cloistered studies carved into Titan bone house row upon row of brittle tomes that line the walls like ossified spines, exhaling dust and decay. Chroniclers spend their days over ancient glyphs—some seeking wisdom, some, like the Inquisitor, seeking only justification. Among the archives lie texts on the Silent God and the primordial balance, descriptions of the Void Wraiths and their eclipse-like mark, and a prophecy in riddles: “God will become man, and man will become God, in the final strife of the sunless storm.”

Militant Training

Beyond pure scholarship, the Spire keeps hunters. The Inquisitor's Eyes of the Spire were seven: Cyrus the interrogator, Elara the master of whispers and infiltration, the trackers Theron and Jorah, the twins Malachi and Jedediah, masters of disguise, and Sister Magdalena, the silent one. Cloaked in austere black, riding stallions black as night and wielding bone-white blades that drink the light, they were dispatched to Solara, the Verdant Veil and the Godscarred Wastes to hunt suspected Bloodmarked—above all the Silver Hunter.

Oppressive Atmosphere

The constant presence of death weighs heavily on residents' minds. Every corridor and chamber is hewn from the skeleton of the Titan, reminding inhabitants daily of mortality and the price of cosmic conflict.

Knowledge Preservation

The Chroniclers preserve dangerous information throughout the bone chambers. This knowledge includes both official doctrine and older, forbidden records that contradict it—texts revealing that the Wild Gods made the Bloodmarked not as abominations but as champions against the Void Wraiths, whose very blood was meant to be a weapon against the rising silence. It was this revelation, read too late, that shattered the Inquisitor's faith.

Surveillance Culture

Daily life operates under constant observation. Sister Magdalena kept her silent vigil within the Spire itself, watching the Chroniclers and acolytes, listening in the libraries and chapels for any flicker of doubt or whisper of dissent. The rigid hierarchy demanded absolute loyalty; the Inquisitor's power was, for a time, absolute.

Architecture of Ancient Death

The Hollow Spire stands as testimony to the marriage of death and purpose, where every street and chamber serves as reminder of cosmic price. Its bone-white heights pierce the sky above crypts choked with the dead, creating a city that exists simultaneously as monument to fallen power and fortress against future threats. Within these bone-carved halls, the boundary between preservation and oppression blurs like shadows cast by structures that gleam bone-white under distant stars. Even the Spire's own contradictions assault the senses: incense over the metallic tang of old blood, cold grandeur, and a silence so heavy it feels alive.

The Libraries

Vast libraries carved into the Titan's bones are said to house the most dangerous knowledge in existence—texts on divine conflict, on the god beyond the pantheon and his Void Wraiths, and on the true making of the Bloodmarked. From the Spire's deepest archives came the forbidden scriptures that spoke of a primordial entity swallowed by silence, and from the Inquisitor's own study the relic hunter's journal that named the Eclipse Pendant of Zenith and the Awakening Tomb.

The Highest Tower

The Spire's highest tower is a solitary chamber open to the wind, its cold bone floor worn by an old man's knees, where the Inquisitor chanted litanies to the Wild Gods as his faith came apart.

The Hidden Waterways

Beneath a hidden cellar door in the dungeon floor a dark, water-filled passage runs out beyond the Spire's walls to the forest—the road by which the Silver Hunter, the Blooming Whisper and the Stray escaped the Inquisitor.

The Ossuary Crypts

Beneath the halls of learning and the chambers of prayer lie the bone-choked crypts, where dust and decay press down and the silence reigns absolute. In a hidden chamber carved directly into the Titan's petrified ribcage, lit only by bone candles, the Eyes of the Spire knelt to receive their missions.

The Dungeons

Subterranean pits of despair hewn from the very bones of the Titan, where iron manacles bite into the wrists of the accused and water drips from cavernous ceilings like a metronome in the lightless dark. Here the Inquisitor pronounced his judgments—and here a chained hunter first told him: “Perhaps you are simply wrong.”
