History & Timeline

"Every age ends in catastrophe, every catastrophe births a new age"

Journey through the chronicle of Arcadia age by age—the Age of Gods, when divine blood spilled; the Great Withdrawal, when the gods fell silent; the Age of Awakening, when silence spoke; the Age of Consequence, after the awakening; and the ages still to be written, when ancient prisons open and foreign gods come ashore. Divine love becomes cosmic catastrophe and mortal hope persists despite overwhelming darkness, as gods and mortals struggle to coexist in a universe too small for infinite love.

🌅
The Age of Gods — The Beginning

The River of First Light

Before the world learned to name itself, the First—the architect-gods who sang substance from the void—each claimed a portion of creation according to their nature. Leika raised the mountains from her breath; Libros inscribed the laws that hold reality in place; Archadeus gave the hunt its hunger; Atonia distilled truth from chaos into beauty that cut like winter wind; and from the spaces between light and substance, Verites whispered darkness into being. Only Thainos, youngest among them, wandered without monuments to his will—until inspiration claimed him beside the River of First Light, a cosmic waterway that ran with a color between dawn and dream, its banks soft with dust fine as ground pearls.

The Divine Creators

Where Libros's angels knew only perfect order, where Archadeus's beastmen knew only primal strength, and where Atonia's elves knew only crystalline truth, Thainos shaped something rarer from the river's clay: he breathed questions into it, wonder, the terrible gift of doubt, the magnificent burden of hope. He gave them softer skin than made sense, hearts that could break from mere words, souls that could contain infinities housed in flesh that lasted mere moments. He made them human. The oldest texts add that the nature spirits who still walk the wild places are Leika's children, and that the shadow-beings were Verites' making. Each race bore the mark of its maker—and each carried the capacity for both profound love and devastating cruelty.

The Age of Gods — When Gods Walked Beside Mortals

When the Gods Walked the World

In the long age that followed creation, gods walked openly among mortals—as healers, guides, wanderers, and lovers. The oldest songs of the Azure coast still recall the time when the land was still called Quenos, "a quiet village by the sea where gods once walked beside mortals," and the sun itself is said to remember when mortals were not afraid of the dark.

Two Kinds of Divinity

Not all the gods walked so close. The First Gods and their younger followers held to their crystalline thrones in the Hall of Eternal Judgment, viewing mortals as mayflies and keeping a cold, hierarchical distance they would one day claim was wisdom. It was the Wild Gods—those who felt emotion with mortal intensity despite cosmic power—who came down among their creations as friends, protectors, and lovers.

The Age of Gods — First Tragedy

The Curse of Sun and Moon

The coastal village of Quenos became ground zero for divine tragedy. Elodias, god of the moon, the tides, and quiet stillness, loved Senia, goddess of the sun, blossoming life, and radiant beauty—and their perfect love drew the obsession of Malritha, a moon-touched seer born hollow during a lunar eclipse. Where other seers received divine visions, none ever spoke to her; she was the vessel, never the visited. From her own emptiness and unrequited longing she wove a curse so perfect that even the gods could not unmake it: Senia, stripped of divinity, is reborn mortal in every life with no memory of what she was, while Elodias keeps his immortality and must find her again—and watch her die within seven days of remembering their true nature.

The Eternal Cycle

Seven days after she remembers, he loses her again. The bards of the coast still sing the story to the dusk, and the names never change. The name of the city that now gleams above the cliffs of old Quenos—Atheria, "carried from the sea"—recalls a great wave that destroyed the old town generations ago; the survivors rebuilt further inland, and still live in terror of the sea's moods. It is told in Seven Days of Sun and The Weaver of Sorrows.

The Age of Gods — The Protection Era

The Guardian Angels

Under Azrael, God of Guardian Angels, a hierarchy of Guardian Angels stood as the shield between the divine and mortal realms—protectors of mortals who, as the old understanding went, did not walk the earth themselves. Azrael held that mortals should not have to live in fear of the divine.

The System's Breakdown

The system did not fail through war but through family. What passed between the guardian angel Azrael and his own kin — and the choice it forced on him — cost him his place among the divine and left the mortal world without its protector; the vacuum he left behind would be filled by predators. Mortals near the village of Oakhaven came to fear a "demon" in the Dark Forest without knowing what it had once been. The whole of that story is told in The Sin of Mercy.

The Age of Gods — The Failed Dream

The Wild Gods Among Mortals

A faction of gods, later called the Wild Gods, believed that mortals deserved to walk among divinity without fear of predation, and that love between infinite and finite beings could be expressed without restraint. Thainos, who made humanity, became mortal himself and walked the earth for a thousand years searching for a single soul untouched by cruelty. Khali, the Dream Shepherd, goddess of dreams, sleep, and the gentle darkness, chose to live as a humble healer by a river. Silas the Trickster, god of change and possibility, watched and waited. And the divine-mortal children born of these ages—the Vaelari, celestial-elven hybrids with starlit eyes; the Haelim, hidden among human populations; the Baelak, divine beastmen so rare they are thought myth—carried power that exceeded their capacity to bear it.

The Cascade

The gods who walked among mortals paid for it. Thainos found compassion everywhere and perfection nowhere, and did not return from his search as he had left it. Khali's murder was rewritten by wealth into a heroic legend carved in stone at High Pass. Azrael was named demon for an act of mercy. And divine words, spoken freely, reshaped existence in unpredictable ways: blessings became curses over time, and every gift became a weapon in mortal hands. Meanwhile, with the guardians gone, other divine beings hunted mortals for sport after dark—until whole villages lived behind shuttered windows from sunset to dawn, and watching the sun go down became a luxury that could cost a life. Their stories are told in The God Who Wept, The Song of Blood and Stone, and the opening of The Divine War.

The Age of Gods — When Divine Blood Spilled

The Divine War

Divine predation reached its breaking point in the mortal villages. When the girl Natalia was torn away by a hunting god during a stolen sunset—five more minutes to watch the light—her friend Astrid's grief opened the door to Silas the Trickster's darkest gift: divine blood, freely offered, that transformed willing mortals into beings capable of challenging heaven itself. Astrid Ravenscroft became the first of the Bloodmarked—mortals who could, for the first time since creation, kill gods. Silas had warned the assembled gods in the Hall of Eternal Judgment that a world so unbalanced would seek its own correction; when they refused to act, he made the correction himself. The Bloodmarked began as volunteers and heroes, thrown flowers in village squares. What they became is why the Ashborn now sing of curses instead of choices.

Cosmic Civil War

The philosophical conflict between the distant First Gods and the Wild Gods who fought for mortals erupted into a civil war that threatened to unmake existence itself. Stars were destroyed, reality cracked under the weight of divine conflict, and gods fell from heaven like burning rain. Their spilled blood seeped into earth, river, and sky, birthing the monstrous Beastborn—manifestations of the gods' wrath, sorrow, and forgotten desires—and leaving the Bloodmarked forever teetering on the edge of corruption, until they could not tell friend from foe. Where the worst of the fighting fell, the land itself died: the Godscarred Wastes, where molten rivers of divine blood still run through cracked earth and the ruins of ancient battles litter the terrain. The chronicle of the war is the final and unfinished tale of The Lost Histories.

The Great Withdrawal — When Gods Fell Silent

The Great Withdrawal

The Divine War ended not with victory but with horror, and its true horror came after. The Wild Gods learned, through losses that would shatter mortal comprehension, that love expressed directly from the infinite to the finite does not elevate—it incinerates. The survivors did not ascend to some distant plane. They withdrew to the deep places of the world—caves that echo with unspoken words, forests where no mortal foot has trod for millennia—and made themselves into living tombs, conscious and aware but forever separate, forever silent save in those rare sanctuaries where divine words can be contained without cracking the cosmos. What remains of the pantheon speaks only there, in voices muffled beneath so many layers of restraint that hearing them requires a lifetime of preparation. The rest are dead or in hiding.

A World Without Answers

Prayers began echoing unanswered into the void—desperate prayers, reaching hands, broken hearts crying out to a heaven that seemed deaf to their suffering. Thirty years into the Withdrawal, when the god-silent dark had become unbearable, the blind star-seer Athea wove a new Common Calendar for merchants and desperate souls alike, naming its months for constellations both visible and prophesied and setting aside the old Arcane Calendar with its eight-day weeks; with it she wove three systems of divination to let humanity feel guided without requiring gods to speak. Only the nature spirits—Leika's children, if the oldest texts are to be believed—remained present in the world as their makers no longer were.

The Great Withdrawal — Truth Buried

The Era of Suppression

In the long silence, truth itself was buried. Knowledge of the Void—the sixth element, beside Spirit and the four primal elements—was banned outright: purged from the realms and deemed heretical, too dangerous to exist. The Hollow Spire itself, in its zeal, branded it anathema, confiscated every text and every fragment, and hunted its very memory from the world—though what the Spire seized, it could not bring itself to destroy. Wealth purchased the rewriting of murder into mythology; the Ashborn of the Wastes turned the Bloodmarked from martyrs into the demons parents use to frighten children. History, as one scholar wrote, is not written by the victors but by the wealthy.

Knowledge Underground

Yet truth survived in what the suppressors overlooked: in the songs the ocean-bound bards still sing to the dusk, in a mirrored script legible only beneath the rising moon, in scrolls sealed in blackwood boxes in sea caves and journals buried under sand in weather-beaten shacks. It was in this era that Tanneus G'Raja, born among the relic-hunters of the Godscarred Wastes and become a Scholar of the Hollow Spire, gathered the fragments that became The Lost Histories—and that an amateur scholar named Alaquinn Althaea began a research journal into the nature spirits, whose behavior had begun to change in troubling ways. A water sprite at Elden Ridge, asked why it no longer cleaned the village well, went still and spoke in a hollow, distant voice: "The Silence… It's coming." Her journal is Voices of the Wild.

The Age of Awakening — When Silence Spoke

The Silence

Long after the pantheon fell quiet, the Silent God—an ancient deity predating the known gods, erased from history and feared even by the divine—awakened from eons of slumber to begin the methodical unmaking of existence. Its whispers reached five mortals across five kingdoms, each word shaped to its hearer's deepest wound: Accept, to Kaelen R'Veyne, the Silver Hunter of the Godscarred Wastes; Emerge, to Princess Elowen Faerwyn of the Verdant Veil; Burn, to Prince Dain Solarius of Solara; Flee, to the Beastman cub Ryx Shadowclaw of the Crimson Tundra; Blind, to High Inquisitor Mordain Ashcroft of the Hollow Spire. Prince Dain, consumed by jealousy of his brother and obsession with Elowen, overthrew Valerius in Solara's arena and whispered his agreement to full possession; the Golden City of Solara, jewel of the south, fell to fire and ash almost overnight, and from its conquered throne the Silent God wore the Crown of Aetheris and commanded legions of Void Wraiths to "Unmake."

The Ultimate Sacrifice

The unlikely fellowship of hunter, princess, cub, and repentant inquisitor sought the Eclipse Pendant in the Awakening Tomb beneath the god-battlefields of the Crimson Tundra, where the Trial of Gods forced each to face their deepest fear. In the miraculously untouched Temple of Solara, Mordain Ashcroft invoked the Angel of Light at the price of complete self-erasure—unmade so thoroughly that even the memory of him faded from those who loved him—and the shockwave of restorative light that swept the realm weakened the Silent God enough for Kaelen, transformed by the Pendant into a being of starlight and fury, to strike off its vessel's head. Elowen poured out every drop of her nature magic to pull him back from divine madness. The cosmic forces the Silent God represented were pushed back, not destroyed; the world was left forever scarred, its blighted regions a permanent reminder of how close creation came to annihilation. This is The Silence.

The Age of Consequence — After the Awakening

The Rise of Zenith's Chosen

Five years after the Silence dropped its curtain, the coastal forest town of Rivenglade had become a haven for refugees from ruined Solara and battered Atheria—and a breeding ground for a new religious order. Lucien Altheris, a Vaelari of the Zenith-worshipping elves of Sommer Isle, arrived preaching the gospel of Zenith's Light and proclaiming himself the "Son of Zenith." His monotheistic movement offered absolute certainty in an uncertain world: the Silence, it taught, was the prophesied end times, the blighted present a tribulation before Zenith's purified reign, and salvation lay only in baptism into Zenith's Chosen. Its doctrine demonized the Wild Gods and the Nimwe—the benevolent void-spirits who arose in numbers after the Silence—and its discipline was the Forsaking, a social death branded into the wrist with a glowing white circle.

The Chosen Chronicles

For Mira Roehart, a young woman of Rivenglade who had always felt like an outsider, the new faith brought not salvation but captivity: forced marriage to the prophet, then a discovery that something ancient in her blood recoiled from his light. Her escape while pregnant leads to sanctuary among the Forsaken, then years of healing in a hidden forest village raising her void-touched son, Kai—and, in the years the Chronicles leave in shadow, exile across the sea on the island of Nareen. Eighteen years after her escape, mother and son return to confront Lucien and reclaim their world. Book One, The Chosen, is published; the story continues in The Forsaken, The Village, The Return, and the companion novel The Girl Beneath the Cherry Tree.

The Age of Broken Seals — When Ancient Prisons Open

The Song of the Dragon

For millennia, a haunting melody has whispered through the minds of the grieving—divine comfort, or so the priests claim. In an age still to be written, the scholar Eren Glavier loses his beloved Raewyn to plague, and the song becomes his obsession. Following a map hidden in its verses, he breaks the seal imprisoning the Dragon, an entity with the power to resurrect anything that has ever died. Raewyn breathes again—but the Dragon's freedom begins resurrecting the Old Gods, who hunger for vengeance, and a divine plague spreads across the world.

A Planned Trilogy

As humanity faces extinction or evolution, Eren must choose between the woman he would damn the world to save and the world he must damn her to protect. The Song of the Dragon—The Mourning Scholar, The Risen Gods, The Final Verse—is a planned trilogy; the details of this age remain in the author's keeping.

The Age of Occupation — When Foreign Gods Came Ashore

The Coming of Hevetica

Across the sea lies the Imperial Republic of Hevetica—a nation of towering, long-lived Bohdkin whose gods never left them, whose weapons are blessed, and whose seers are infused with divine magic through ritual. They believe their order is superior. They believe Arcadia—a land that has nearly ended the world more than once—cannot be trusted to govern itself. So, in the age after the Song of the Dragon, they come to govern it.

Godless Savages

Senach is the Bohdkin word for an Arcadian: godless savage. But in a universe where mortals have already killed gods, an empire whose power is borrowed rather than born has more to fear than it knows. The Senach—a story about occupation, faith as infrastructure, and what the abandoned still carry in their blood—is in development, and everything about this age beyond its premise remains unwritten.
