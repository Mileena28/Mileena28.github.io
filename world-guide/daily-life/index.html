Daily Life & Culture

"How mortals endure when gods hunt in darkness and survival becomes the highest art"

In the Arcadia of the old songs, every sunset brought terror and every dawn offered fragile hope; the sun itself is said to remember when mortals were not afraid of the dark. Mortal communities learned to live under the shadow of divine predation, developing intricate survival strategies, protective traditions, and social bonds forged in the crucible of shared vulnerability. Here, culture emerges not from leisure but from necessity, and the deepest human connections are those that keep the darkness at bay.

Survival Under Divine Predation

When Gods Hunted Mortals for Sport

In the last days of the Age of Gods, after the Guardian Angels lost their commander, mortal communities existed in perpetual terror, structuring their entire existence around avoiding divine attention. Every aspect of daily life was calculated to survive in a world where certain gods viewed humans as prey rather than subjects worthy of protection—the world of Astrid and Natalia's village on the eve of the Divine War.

Daily Survival Protocols

All mortal activity had to cease when darkness fell, as divine predators became active after dark; families lay behind locked doors and shuttered windows while "the night continued its hunt." Simple pleasures like watching a sunset from a meadow became dangerous luxuries that could cost lives—"five more minutes" was all Astrid asked, and it cost her dearest friend. Mornings, by contrast, belonged to the sacred grove and the dawn gathering, where an elder led the invocation to the earth spirits.

Essential Protocols:
  • Sunset curfews behind locked doors and shuttered windows
  • Sunsets as deadly luxuries—"five more minutes"
  • Dawn gatherings in the sacred grove with invocations to the earth spirits

Community Processing of Trauma

Mortal communities developed elaborate traditions for processing frequent violent deaths and supernatural threats. Bodies were burned on pyres of carefully arranged pine logs, the scent of cedar and ceremonial herbs over the smell of death, while hollow-eyed villagers who had gathered too many times in the same square spoke too many prayers over too many pyres.

Coping Mechanisms:
  • Pyre ceremonies with pine logs and ceremonial herbs
  • Collective mourning where entire villages gather in the square

Community Social Structures

Bonds Forged in Shared Vulnerability

From crossroads hamlets to towns with their own statutes, the settlements of Arcadia keep their own kinds of order.

Village Governance Systems

Small settlements like Oakhaven grow up around a crossroads and an inn, wary of strangers and quick to make legends of what lives in their woods. Larger towns grow their own bureaucracies: at Elden Ridge, a Municipal Authority of magistrate, scribe, and warden captain fines unauthorized researchers fifteen silver pieces a day under Municipal Statute 847-B, while its wardens keep watch over the sacred animals that bear divine markings.

Governance Elements:
  • Elden Ridge's Municipal Authority: magistrate, scribe, and warden captain
  • Municipal Statute 847-B and its fifteen-silver-a-day fines
  • Wardens who guard the sacred animals bearing divine markings

Marriage & Family Life

Intimacy Under Divine Surveillance

Marriage and family structures reflect the pressures of living under supernatural threats. From the controlled marriages of Zenith's Chosen to the private lullabies of Nareen, intimate relationships become both refuge and vulnerability in a dangerous world.

Zenithian Marriage Control

Under Zenith's Chosen, marriage becomes a tool of social control and organizational expansion. The bride is constrained in white and gold before the whole congregation; the prophet's vows are ownership disguised as devotion—"from this day forward, you are mine… I am your home, your refuge, your purpose"—and sermons preach the "divinely ordained roles of wives." Ceremonies emphasize absolute submission, domestic surveillance in which spouses report one another for signs of doubt, and the production of new generations of faithful followers.

Control Mechanisms:
  • Public spectacles emphasizing submission and obedience themes
  • Spousal surveillance and reporting on spiritual compliance
  • Social isolation from relationships outside the faith community
  • Complete bodily autonomy surrender expected from wives

Child-Rearing Under Threat

Raising children in Arcadia means raising them in a world where divine predation is real—and where a child's Bloodmarked nature may be a death sentence: the Ashborn of the Wastes teach their children to check for signs of the curse and exile any who show the mark, while the Bloodfang Clans of the Crimson Tundra offer their most potent Bloodmarked children on frozen altars beneath the Blood Moon. Ryx Shadowclaw fled that altar only because his mother took his place.

Parenting Challenges:
  • Early recognition of potential Bloodmarked heritage
  • Ashborn children taught to check for signs of the mark; the marked exiled
  • Bloodfang cubs of potent Bloodmarked power offered beneath the Blood Moon

Love and Kinship on Nareen

Beyond the mist, on the island of Nareen, love is kept differently. The Nareenese are people of quiet humor, patient temperaments, and deep emotional loyalty who believe that words carry resonance beyond speech; love songs and lullabies are sung in private rather than in public, meant to "anchor" the soul of a loved one and keep it from wandering. Every spring, a wish for love, health, or prosperity may be carved into the base of a cherry tree—and a promise once carved into bark can never be taken back.

Nareenese Customs:
  • Lullabies and love songs sung privately to anchor a loved one's soul
  • A cherry tree planted for every birth of the previous year
  • Wishes and names carved into cherry bark, taken with utmost seriousness
  • Careful, deliberate, often poetic speech
  • Homes scented with salt air, sandalwood, and flower water

Economic Life Under Supernatural Influence

Commerce in the Shadow of Divine Chaos

Trade and commerce in Arcadia have had to reckon with divine catastrophe—from the silence of the gods to the blight the Silence left behind.

A Calendar for Merchants

It was for merchants and desperate souls alike that the blind star-seer Athea wove the Common Calendar thirty years into the Great Withdrawal, giving commerce a practical count of days after the gods fell silent.

Athea's Common Calendar:
  • Woven thirty years into the Great Withdrawal by the blind star-seer Athea
  • Months named for constellations, replacing the Arcane Calendar and its eight-day weeks
  • Three systems of divination woven alongside it

Post-Crisis Economic Adaptation

Following major supernatural crises like the Silence, economic systems must rapidly adapt to changed circumstances. Atheria, the breadbasket kingdom, saw its fields blighted by Beastborn and void-corruption and turned to its fishing fleets; ruined Solara's people fled as refugees; and Rivenglade, living on coastal fishing and the forest, absorbed migrants from both.

Crisis Adaptation:
  • Atheria's blighted heartland and its turn to the fishing fleets
  • Solaran refugees in Rivenglade and Atheria
  • Rivenglade's coastal fishing and forest economy absorbing migrants

Cultural Adaptation & Traditions

Preserving Humanity While Embracing Change

From the silent shrines of Arcadia to the festivals of Nareen, communities keep faith and tradition alive in a world where the gods no longer answer.

Religious & Spiritual Evolution

Spiritual practices adapt to accommodate both traditional Wild God worship and new movements like Zenith's Chosen. Despite apparent divine abandonment, communities keep up prayers that seem to go unanswered; a village may keep its well sweet with offerings of bread and honey cakes to a water sprite, and the Dawnseekers still hear the Wild Gods whisper in the Verdant Veil.

Spiritual Adaptation:
  • Prayers kept up though they seem to go unanswered
  • Hidden preservation of Wild God traditions among the Forsaken
  • Offerings of bread and honey cakes to Elden Ridge's water sprite
  • Dawnseekers who still hear the Wild Gods in the Veil

The Four Festivals of Nareen

On the island of Nareen, whose people believe each part of the land houses a resting spirit and that to disturb balance is to wake sorrow, the turning of the seasons is marked by four sacred festivals—living covenants between the people, the land, and the divine. The Nareenese and the sirens of their coasts keep a sacred pact: offerings of fish after storms, seashell tombs for any siren washed ashore, and the white-haired blessed of Maiku who alone may speak with them.

Seasonal Rites:
  • Spring Rite of Blossoms (Luralei): a cherry tree planted for every birth; wishes carved into bark
  • Summer Wind Festival (Chiru): thousands of chimes hung to carry the voices of the lost home
  • Autumn Festival of Mist (Oruen): the silent pilgrimage to the Vale of Steps with prayers on bark paper
  • Winter Sea Festival (Maiku): the offering exchange with the sirens—food for deep water, black sand, and shells
  • Nightly mist, when the spirits move between dreams and waking
