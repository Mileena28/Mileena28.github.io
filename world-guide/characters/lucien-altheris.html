When Divine Beauty Masks Cosmic Evil

He came "in the pause between breaths." Five years after the Silence, with refugees washing onto Rivenglade's pier, Lucien Altheris arrived like "a fallen star… shorn of its destructive corona yet still possessing a luminous core." His eyes were "impossibly blue," holding "a radiance that did not merely reflect light but conjured it"; his voice was "smooth as polished stone and rich as aged honey wine," a tangible force that snuffed out doubt before it could take root. He did not walk the cobblestones so much as glide. And he spoke not of gods, but of God: Zenith. Singular. Supreme. Absolute.

To the town he is a savior; to the refugees, a king; to Mira Roehart, a jailer. Within a season Rivenglade's marketplace pulses with hushed reverence, craftsmen carve Zenithian geometry into their wares, hymns drift from repurposed buildings, and neighbours wear "a calm bordering on vacant." Lucien builds a hierarchy — Faithful Elders, Devout Overseers, Messengers of the Light — declares a crusade to carry the Light beyond Rivenglade, teaches the faithful to sever themselves from the "unclean," and turns spouses into informants. His sermons never threaten; they simply present his vision as inevitability "while dismantling every alternative."

Beneath the sculpted serenity is something the book names early and Mira sees on the altar: "a flicker of something cold behind his luminous gaze. Something dark. Something inhuman." What begins as a shepherd's patient manipulation of one doubting girl becomes, once his Light fails to take her, coercion, ownership, marital rape and gaslighting — abuse dressed as devotion, cruelty framed as mercy. His divinity, the back cover says, "does not purify her. It recoils."

You are already walking toward the Light, Mira. Whether you realize it or not. Your questions, your doubts, your very presence here—all of it is simply the first step on your destined path.
— Lucien to Mira (The Chosen, Chapter 4)
Story in Progress
The Chosen is published in full; of The Forsaken, only Chapters 23–24 are public. This profile stops there. The author's series pages describe Lucien as a Vaelari — "corruption wearing the face of divinity" — and set the whole four-book arc as Mira's eighteen-year road back to confront him. How that confrontation goes is not written here.

The Fallen Angel's Twisted Heart

Lucien's horror lies not in rage but in composure. He watches a man stoned to death in the square with "clinical neutrality," neither stopping nor encouraging it, then says only, "He chose the woods. Let the woods have him." He apologises to Mira in order to violate her more intimately, and calls her "good girl" afterward. He rarely raises his voice — until the night she runs.

Certainty as a Weapon
"He'd never explicitly demanded she join. Never threatened or coerced. He'd simply presented his vision as inevitability, offering the illusion of choice while dismantling every alternative" (Ch. 4).
Twisted Righteousness
Rape is "wifely duty" and "consummation"; a threatened murder is "mercy"; a public lynching is a lesson in vigilance. Every cruelty arrives wrapped in scripture and honeyed patience (Ch. 9–11, 16).
The Patient Hunter
He visits her father, opens her trinket box, lets the feather hum on his desk, and waits: "the quiet satisfaction of a hunter who had meticulously, patiently cornered his prey" (Ch. 11). Silence is his favourite tool.
Something He Cannot Place
Her immunity unsettles him. On their wedding night he studies her eyes: "There's something unique about you… Something I can't quite place yet." Later: "Something I recognize." He does not know until the void burns him (Ch. 14, 21–22).
Mira, my beloved, from this day forward, you are mine, as I am yours… I am your home, your refuge, your purpose.
— Lucien's wedding vow (The Chosen, Chapter 13)

Light Weaponized Against the Innocent

Zenith's Light is real, and it is his. What the congregation sees as divine grace is, from inside, "a presence. Cold. Alien. Other" — a force that "wanted to rewrite her." The author's series page is blunter still: the Baptism is "not mere ritual but supernatural mind control," and only Mira's Nimwe blood is immune.

The Baptism
The Ritual: The initiate walks barefoot in a sheer gown, lies on the marble altar, and swears the oath — "I give my body, mind, and soul to Zenith… All Hail Zenith!" (Ch. 8)
The Light: White radiance blooms from his palm into the forehead; the body lifts from the slab, "a puppet dangling from unseen strings"
The Rewriting: Inside, a force threads through doubt and self, "to forge her into something obedient. Something compliant. Something Chosen"
The Result: Serene, uniform, synthetic joy — and, in the words of a man freed from it, a fog (Forsaken Ch. 24)
The Forsaking
The Brand: Light swirling with divine script narrows to a point and sears "a perfect, unbroken white circle" into the wrist — "through the soul itself" (Ch. 10)
Social Death: "From this day forward, he is already dead. Spiritually. Irrevocably. Eternally… Forsaken" (Ch. 9)
The Mob: Stripped, spat on, stoned in the square while their leader watches
The Marked: Every Forsaken in Meridia's sanctuary bears his circle; Meridia bares hers with "defiant pride" (Forsaken Ch. 24)
Sensing and Seizing
Magic Does Not Hide: "I feel it. I know it." The Nimwe feather called to him from Mira's room and hummed at his touch (Ch. 11)
Reading Souls: He names Mira's deepest fears within minutes of meeting her, and turns every question back on the questioner (Ch. 4)
Isolation: Elias to Atheria, Kat to Blackwater Falls — "a strategic decision… It had nothing to do with you, my love" (Ch. 15–16)
Gaslighting: Grief reframed as selfishness; leaving as sin; his cruelty as her failure
Void Vulnerability
Baptism Failure: "This had never happened before… His Light had always been absolute, irresistible. It did not falter. It did not fail" — until Mira (Ch. 8)
Burned: Black tendrils from her arm sink into his wrist "like molten acid"; he screams "a raw, inhuman shriek," smoke curling from seared flesh (Ch. 22)
Recognition: The feather, the resistance, the energy — "the puzzle finally, terrifyingly complete": "She's Nimwe."
Reversal: The same void that repels him lifts his fog from others (Forsaken Ch. 24)

From Seduction to Systematic Destruction

Every step of Lucien's pursuit of Mira is on the page of The Chosen. Read in order, it is a manual of coercive control with a god's light behind it.

The Predator's Campaign
The Shepherd's Voice (Ch. 1–4)
"Mira Roehart. I wondered when you would come." He answers none of her questions and names all of her fears; she leaves "paradoxically lighter—as if part of herself had been imperceptibly stolen."
Closing the Circle (Ch. 6–8)
He converts Elias in a single conversation, preaches isolation from the "unclean," and — "I always knew you were meant to walk this path" — baptises Mira. His Light recoils. "He knew that she knew."
Lesson and Ultimatum (Ch. 9–11)
A man who met the Nimwe is Forsaken and stoned; Lucien's eyes find Mira over the body: "Such is the fate of those who listen to the Nimwe." Then, feather on the desk: marry me, or it is found in Kat's home.
Possession (Ch. 13–14)
Vows of ownership; a forehead kiss "seared not onto her body, but into her mind"; a sterile house with none of her things, where he undresses her, studies her violet eyes, and turns away.
Isolation and Violation (Ch. 15–19)
Father and friend removed. "It is expected, Mira… That we consummate our marriage." Nights of rape; a forced return to public life; a false apology used as a new violation; "Good girl."
The Hunt Begins (Ch. 21–22)
He senses something changed in her and forgives her the forest "this time." When his Hall explodes and he finds her with a satchel: "Run after her! Don't let her escape! Stop her!" Her void answers. "She's Nimwe."
The Cosmic Abuser
Lucien's Light promises to swallow the Silence — and it does swallow: doubt, shadow, nuance, warmth. Rivenglade trades one extreme for another, "a deafening roar of devotion" for the silence that came before. The Nimwe's warning to Mira is the book's verdict on him: "Too much light… will make you blind. When you cannot see, how will you guide yourself? You will stumble. You will falter. And inevitably, blindly… you will follow."
Such is the fate of those who listen to the Nimwe.
— Lucien, over the body of a Forsaken man (The Chosen, Chapter 10)

The False Light That Devours Truth

Lucien Altheris is the answer Rivenglade was praying for and the worst thing that ever happened to it. He arrives with clarity where there was chaos and certainty where there was doubt, and the battered town turns toward him "yearning for the salvation it so alluringly offered." Only Mira, on her solitary perch at the end of the pier, feels the chilling undercurrent: "something vital being surrendered."

He is a Vaelari, a being of elven and angelic blood, and the Light he wields is genuine — genuine enough to lift a body from a marble slab, to burn a circle through skin and soul, to hum a Nimwe feather awake in his fingers. What he does with it is the book's indictment. He rewrites the willing and brands the unwilling; he turns wives into informants and children into strangers to their fathers; he uses a friend's life as the price of a marriage and a marriage as the licence for rape. Every act is delivered in "honeyed persuasion," and every act leaves him serene.

His one failure is the hinge of the whole series. On the altar his Light "struck a wall" in Mira Roehart, and for the first time in all his years as the Son of Zenith he did not win. He hid it from the congregation and could not hide it from her; from that moment the shepherd becomes a hunter, and the hunt ends — for now — with his own flesh smoking and a single word of recognition: Nimwe.

Beyond Chapter 24 of The Forsaken, the author has published only the outline: The Son of Zenith's Light spreads, his fled wife carries his child, and mother and son return after eighteen years "to confront Lucien and the oppressive grip of Zenith's Light." Whether the False Light can be put out is the question the Chosen Chronicles exist to answer.
