When Truth Returns with Liberation

He enters the Chosen Chronicles as a sack over a head. Dominic and Jay drag him into the Forsaken's cavern in tatters of white and gold, "tall, broad-shouldered, yet diminished," bind him to a chair, and prop the thing they took from him against the wall — "a longsword in a battered leather scabbard, a faded gold sun worked into the pommel." When Meridia tears the hood away, the face beneath is "weathered, sun-browned, carved by wind and hard roads," and the first thing it does is pray: "Zenith's Light, shine upon me… keep the void from me… in Yer Radiance I am made whole…"

He will not be reasoned out of the Light. Meridia tells him Lucien "gave you a stillness and called it peace"; he answers that he remembers the man he was before — "Starvin'. Prayin' to gods that had gone quiet… I was nobody. Waitin' to die with the rest of 'em. And Lucien found me. He gave me my life back." When reason yields to Dominic's fists, the trembling convert becomes someone else entirely: he rolls the ache from his jaw like a laborer, spits blood between Dominic's boots and asks, low and even, "That all ya got for me, pretty boy?" Fists, the book observes, "were a country he knew."

Then Mira's void fills the cavern, and something passes through him that has nothing to do with the beating. His blown pupils shiver and steady "like a man stepping out of blinding noon into shade." "Everything's gone… foggy," he tells her. "But… the fog… it's liftin'." The public chapters end there. The author's own series page tells the rest in outline: a former Solarian soldier "whose liberation from Zenithian conditioning proves that authentic consciousness can triumph over manufactured compliance," who becomes Mira's found father — and whose name her son will carry.

Story in Progress
Only Chapters 23–24 of The Forsaken are public, and this profile stops there. Mordekai's story is the heart of the road that follows — Mira's road out of the sanctuary and toward the Verdant Veil. What he remembers of Solara, what he swears to her, and what it costs him are for the book to tell. Read it before you read anything else about him.

Bearer of Truth and Loyalty

Two men sit in that chair in Chapter 24: the convert who prays through his tears, and the soldier who takes a beating "like penance" and chuckles. Both are him. The Light did not erase the second man; it only put him to sleep.

Loyal to a Fault
He is loyal to Lucien in the chair because Lucien found him starving. The series page names where that loyalty goes once his mind is his own: to Mira, as the father she never had.
Witness to the Silence
"Ya remember what it was like, after the Silence. Ya must. The whole world come apart at the seams." He was in Solara when it fell — the "sun-browned" face and the sun on his sword both say so (Ch. 24).
Clear Sight, Returning
"His pupils shivered, contracted, steadied." He looks at Mira "not… the way he had looked at his captors": with confusion, hesitation, "a fragile spark of wonder" (Ch. 24).
Built to Endure
One eye swelling shut, blood webbing from his nose, he keeps coming back up — "Slower now… but up. Still up." He rides the blows and will not give Dominic the collapse he wants (Ch. 24).
That all ya got for me, pretty boy?
— The captive to Dominic (The Forsaken, Chapter 24)

The Gift of Clear Sight
The captive in the chair is the series' first proof that Zenith's Light can be undone — not by argument, which Meridia tries and abandons, nor by pain, which Dominic tries and cannot stop, but by the void itself. "The fog… it's liftin'." What that lifting makes of a Solarian soldier, and what he makes of the girl who lifted it, gives The Forsaken its heart. The author's dedication for the book — "for the orphans with living parents… for the parent who chooses me" — is the shape of it.

The Voice of Liberated Memory

Mordekai arrives in the Chosen Chronicles without a name and nearly without a face — a Zenithian in torn robes, a sack over his head, a sword with a sun on it that a young Forsaken dismisses as loot. For most of Chapter 24 he is a demonstration: the Forsaken's argument against the Light, made on a man who will not stop praying, and then a man who will not stop chuckling through his blood.

What the chapter quietly establishes is that Lucien's Light was laid over someone. Beneath the "worn smoothness" of the prayers is a soldier who has been hit before, who survived Solara's fall and starved after it, who took the Light because it was offered when nothing else was. Meridia's contempt — "Every one of them. Every single one, the same" — is answered by the moment Mira's void passes through him and the man beneath looks up.

The author has said, in outline, who that man becomes: the "Liberated Witness," former Solarian soldier, Mira's found father, the name she gives her son. The dedication of The Forsaken — to "the parent who chooses me—not from obligation or blood, but from recognition" — is written for him as much as for anyone. Everything between the chair and that dedication is the book's, and it is worth every page.
