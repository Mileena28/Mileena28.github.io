When Love Becomes the Greatest Betrayal

Elias Roehart raised his daughter alone in a house of woodsmoke and old books, and he called her little starling. He is the steady island of The Chosen's early chapters — the man in his usual corner by the window, "immersed in the pages of a thick, leather-bound volume," who has "never been a man of excess words" and who kept his careful distance from Lucien's rising fervor: "neither approval nor condemnation, neutral, detached, a passive resistance in a town steadily surrendering to Lucien's light."

He is also the wall Mira has spent her life pushing against. Every question about her mother meets the same locked door — "She simply couldn't, Mira" — until, cornered, he shouts the only true thing he ever tells her: "Because she's not like us, Mira! Can't you understand? She walks a different path—a path we can never follow." What else he knows about the woman who left, and about the violet-eyed child she left him, the published book never says.

Then, one morning over oatcakes and herb tea: "I spoke with Lucien last night… And I think I understand now." A single conversation takes the anchor out from under Mira; the same devotion that makes him hand Lucien the run of the house — and, unknowing, the Nimwe feather in her trinket box — makes him proud to be "chosen" for Atheria the day after her wedding. He leaves with a promise to write "every week, if not more," blind to the cage he is leaving her in.

Mira… oh, little starling. You are perfect, exactly as you are. In every way that matters.
— Elias to Mira (The Chosen, Chapter 2)
Story in Progress
The Chosen is published in full; Elias's part in it ends on the water. Whether his letters ever reach his daughter — and what they might say — is a matter for The Forsaken, and this profile does not go there.

The Heart That Chose Silence Over Truth

Elias's love is never in doubt; the book gives it to us in a hand cupping a cheek, a firelit lap, a forehead kiss. What the book withholds is everything else — and the withholding is his.

Fierce Paternal Love
"Don't squander your time on this earth fretting over what others think… What matters is who you are within. The fire in your heart, the strength in your spirit, and the truth that burns inside you" (Ch. 2).
Quiet Reader
A collection of old paper and well-worn leather; ancient tomes Mira takes for "forgotten lore"; the calm of a man who lives in his corner by the window (Ch. 2).
Secret Keeper
"Why is it always 'better this way'?" Mira throws his refrain back at him. His face hardens into "the familiar mask of impenetrable silence—a fortress against painful memories" (Ch. 2).
Won in a Night
"I've been thinking about your future… In Zenith's Chosen, there is safety. Purpose. Stability. You wouldn't have to fight so hard to belong." For a moment "the ghost of his former self" flickers — then he shakes it off (Ch. 6).
Because she's not like us, Mira! Can't you understand? She walks a different path—a path we can never follow.
— Elias, on Mira's mother (The Chosen, Chapter 2)

Wisdom Corrupted by Fear and Isolation

Elias has no power but the one every parent has: to shape what a child believes about herself. He used it well, and then he handed it to Lucien.

Unconditional Love
"Little starling": A childhood endearment, "a rare relic," that returns at her wedding and at his farewell (Ch. 2, 13, 15)
Firelight: Mira remembers "a little girl, curled in his lap, eyes wide with wonder as he read to her by firelight" (Ch. 15)
Down the Aisle: His hand squeezes hers when her feet will not move; he hesitates, "barely perceptible," before passing her hand to Lucien (Ch. 13)
Farewell: "I love you so much, Mira." His tears against her temple; his hands framing her face "as if memorizing every detail" (Ch. 15)
A Reader's Life
The Collection: Old paper and worn leather perfume the house; dried herbs hang from the rafters (Ch. 2)
Old Stories: Mira grew up "steeped in their stories" — the woodland spirits and sea deities of the Wild Gods — in his house (Ch. 1)
Skeptic's Distance: Until Chapter 6 he neither attends nor condemns the sermons; his silence was Mira's comfort
Packed With Care: Her books, her clothes, a linen nightgown — everything of hers, delivered to Lucien's door (Ch. 15–16)
What He Would Not Say
The Mother: Absent since Mira's infancy; "She simply couldn't"; "not like us"; a different path mortals cannot follow (Ch. 2)
The Eyes: He never explains the violet that marks his daughter as "striking, unnatural, undeniably other"
"Better this way": His lifelong answer, which Mira calls "hollow"
Unwitting Betrayal: "Eager to serve Zenith's Light," he told Lucien where to find her — and let him into her room (Ch. 11)
Chosen for Atheria
The Assignment: "To spread Zenith's Light there… help restore the city, the farmlands… bring order to the chaos" (Ch. 15)
Atheria: "The battered breadbasket kingdom, still struggling to rebuild in the wake of the Silence"
The Timing: The morning after the wedding, the same day Kat is sent west — Lucien's "strategic decision" (Ch. 16)
The Promise: "I will write to you often. Every week, if not more."

From Protective Love to Tragic Abandonment

Elias's fall is quiet and quick — four scenes in a published book, each one taking something from his daughter.

The Scholar's Fall
The Corner by the Window (Ch. 2)
"Why am I so different from everyone else?" Tenderness, then the locked door, then the outburst: "not like us." Mira retreats to a cracked mirror and asks it, Who am I?
Passive Resistance (Ch. 1–5)
While Rivenglade turns toward the Light, Elias keeps to his books. His neutrality is "a steady anchor in the shifting tides of faith."
"I spoke with Lucien last night" (Ch. 6)
One conversation. "He looked like a man who had already made a choice." He asks Mira to sermon "together, as a family," and the trap is complete.
Accommodating (Ch. 11)
He greets Lucien at the door, "eager to serve Zenith's Light," and the Son of Zenith walks out with the Nimwe feather that will be Mira's leash.
Down the Aisle (Ch. 13)
"My little starling. You are radiant." Words "so full of a father's love" that land "like a eulogy spoken over the dead." He gives her hand away.
Farewells (Ch. 15–16)
Sacks of her belongings at Lucien's door; "I… I have been chosen." A last embrace, a promise of letters, and a sail swallowed by the Azure Expanse.
The Price of Protective Love
Elias never learns what happens in his daughter's marriage. He sees the strong woman he raised and not the terror behind her brittle smile — and Mira, "trapped in careful deception," will not break his faith with the truth. His silence about her mother left her unarmed for the question of what she is; his surrender to the Light handed her jailer the key. Neither was malice. That is what makes it unbearable.
I am so proud of you. Of the strong woman you have become.
— Elias's farewell to Mira before sailing for Atheria (The Chosen, Chapter 15)

The Tragedy of Love Without Truth

Elias Roehart is a good father in every scene he is given, and his goodness is not enough. He gave Mira a childhood of firelight and Wild God stories and the certainty that she was "perfect, exactly as you are" — and he gave her nothing at all about the woman who left her, except that she "walks a different path." The violet eyes that make his daughter a stranger in her own town are a subject he closes with a hardened jaw and the phrase she has come to hate: better this way.

His conversion is the hinge of The Chosen's first act. So long as the skeptical reader in the corner held out, Mira could believe there was room outside the Light; when he comes to breakfast sounding "like one of them," the choice, as she says, "didn't feel like a choice at all." From there his love works against her without his knowledge: he lets Lucien into the house, tells him where to find her, delivers her belongings to a marriage he thinks is an honor, and sails for Atheria the day after, proud of the strong woman he does not realize is drowning.

The book refuses to make him a villain. His hesitation at the altar is real; his tears at the farewell are real; his promise of weekly letters is made in good faith. He simply cannot see — and Mira, who could tell him, chooses to protect his faith rather than break it. Father and daughter part on a lie of kindness, and the Azure Expanse takes him north.

What Elias knew, what he wrote, and whether his words ever find his little starling are threads that The Forsaken picks up. Here, in the published book, he remains what he was on the first day: the man by the window with a book in his lap, who loved his daughter and could not tell her who she was.
