The Girl Who Checked the Wrist

Ezzie steps forward before Gerald's suspicion has time to fester. She is slender and pale, barely more than a girl, "yet there was nothing fragile in the way she moved" — something sharp about her, "keen-edged and coiled tight," her bright eyes fixed on the stranger Wren has brought into the sanctuary. Without warning she lunges, clamps Mira's right wrist "with iron strength," and wrenches the sleeve up so hard the fabric rips. Mira recoils, breath ragged, shielding the bared skin as if it were a wound. Ezzie pays the panic no mind. She is looking for the mark the Zenith cult brands on the right wrist of everyone it casts out — and it is not there.

"No mark," she says flatly. "She ain't Forsaken. She ain't one of us." It is the sanctuary's first hard fact about Mira, and it sends a fresh wave of murmurs through the cavern: if she is not Forsaken, why is she here, and why is Lucien hunting her with such zeal?

The next day she is back — a shadow falling across Mira as she turns Kat's wooden starling over in her palm. Ezzie squats beside her without a word, her pale eyes locked on the carving, and then snatches it, spinning it between her slender fingers, dancing just out of reach each time Mira pleads or grabs, "as if testing Mira's limits." It takes Wren's quick hand to pluck the starling back and toss it into Mira's lap; Ezzie's face contorts into a petulant frown, her scorn aimed solely at Wren, and she stalks off into the deeper dark. "I'm sorry 'bout Ezzie," Wren drawls. "She's… well, she's not quite right in the head, if ya catch my drift… She don't mean ya no harm. Just… a bit touched by the Void, poor lass."

No mark. She ain't Forsaken. She ain't one of us.
— Ezzie, having ripped Mira's sleeve to look (Chapter 23)
Where the Story Stands
The Chosen (Book One) is complete and published; of The Forsaken, Chapters 23 and 24 are online. Everything on this page comes from those two chapters. The sanctuary has uses for Ezzie beyond staring, and what "touched by the Void" means for her is a question the later chapters take up.

Keen-Edged and Coiled Tight

The chapters describe Ezzie almost entirely through motion and gaze. She does not explain herself; she acts, stares, and withdraws. Wren's explanation is the only one anyone offers, and it is offered gently — a finger twirled near the temple, and "poor lass."

Unsettling Fascination
In Chapter 24 she lurks at a distance, "her pale eyes gleaming with unsettling fascination, as if she'd just discovered a particularly interesting new specimen to dissect." Mira, finding her paired with Gerald: "Fantastic. Now they're multiplying."
Birdlike Motion
"Sharp and angular" when she squats; "quick, erratic, almost birdlike" when she pivots away with the starling; "stiff with indignation" when she stalks off.
Flat Assessment
One line in two chapters, and it is a verdict: "No mark. She ain't Forsaken. She ain't one of us." Her silence, the text says, is "more unnerving than any accusation."
Testing Limits
She keeps the starling "always just beyond Mira's grasp… a cruel game played with impish precision," her expression unreadable — until Wren ends it, and her scorn finds a target.
She don't mean ya no harm. Just… a bit touched by the Void, poor lass.
— Wren, to Mira, after retrieving the starling (Chapter 24)

What the Chapters Show

Ezzie is given no powers, no history and no explanation in the two public chapters — only Wren's phrase, and the world it belongs to. What follows sets down what the text shows and what the wider world of the series makes of it.

The Mark She Looks For
The Forsaking: Lucien presses a glowing palm to the outcast's exposed wrist — "A perfect, unbroken white circle seared itself into the man's wrist… A mark of eternal exile" (The Chosen, Ch. 10)
Her Test: right wrist, sleeve wrenched up, fabric ripping
Her Finding: "No mark"
Its Weight: "The words echoed in the confined space, heavy with unspoken questions"
"Touched by the Void"
Wren's Diagnosis: "not quite right in the head" — "a bit touched by the Void"
Wren's Reassurance: "She don't mean ya no harm"
The Irony: the woman she pronounces "not one of us" is, by Lucien's own accusation, Nimwe — a creature of the void — and proves it before the chapter is out
Left Open: what the phrase means for Ezzie herself
The Starling
What It Is: a small wooden starling carved by Kat, Mira's soul-sister from Rivenglade — "Kat's gift"
What Ezzie Does: squats, stares, snatches, twirls, dances away
Who Ends It: Wren, "faster than Mira could track"
Aftermath: Mira tucks the starling back into her satchel and lets her hand rest there
Her Place in the Sanctuary
Accepted: the Forsaken let her act; Wren apologises for her rather than scolding her
Watched Over: "poor lass" — Wren's protectiveness is casual and complete
Watching: she becomes, with Gerald, one of Mira's "opposing sentinels"
Watching Still: her vigil is the last the chapter shows of her before Mira's void tears through the cavern

Two Encounters

Ezzie's part in the public chapters is two brief encounters with Mira, both physical, both uninvited, and one line of dialogue.

The carving plucked from her fingers "like a struck spark" and tossed back. A petulant frown, scorn for Wren, and she stalks off into the dark.
The Sentinel
Later, lurking at a distance with her pale eyes gleaming — Mira's second watcher, opposite Gerald's perch.
Wren's Kindness
The chapter is careful with Ezzie even when Ezzie is not careful with Mira. Wren intervenes without anger, apologises for the girl rather than to her, and gives Mira the only explanation on offer with a chuckle and a twirled finger. Whatever "touched by the Void" turns out to mean, the sanctuary's first response to it is not fear but "poor lass."

— Wren, "her slang-laced accent softening the abrupt intrusion" (Chapter 24)

The Pale Girl in the Dark

Ezzie is the sanctuary's strangest welcome. Where Gerald offers Mira suspicion and Meridia offers her calculation, Ezzie offers a ripped sleeve, a flat verdict and, the next day, a game she plays with the one object Mira has left from home. She is "barely more than a girl," and nothing about her is soft: the chapters give her iron strength in her grip, a stare like a dissecting knife, and movements the text keeps calling birdlike.

Her single line — "No mark. She ain't Forsaken. She ain't one of us" — does more work than any speech in the chamber. It is what makes Mira an unknown rather than a refugee, and it is Ezzie, not Gerald, who establishes it. That the woman she rules out is, by Lucien's own accusation, a creature of the void — and that Ezzie herself is, in Wren's phrase, "a bit touched by the Void" — is an irony the text leaves lying on the cavern floor.

What the phrase means, and what the sanctuary relies on her for, belong to chapters of The Forsaken that are not yet online. In the two that are, she is what Mira sees: a shadow that falls across her, a pair of pale eyes gleaming from a distance, and Wren's easy, protective "poor lass."
