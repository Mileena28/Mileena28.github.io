"The Lost Soul-Sister"

Human

Status (as of The Chosen)
Baptized Zenithian; "chosen to relocate" to Blackwater Falls
Last Seen

Sacred Gift
Sketching, carving; the steady faith of a soul-sister

When Gentle Hearts Choose Extremes

"Kat had always been Mira's anchor, the steady counterbalance to her fire—a devout presence shaped by Rivenglade's traditions, embodying quiet virtue and steadfast faith." She is the girl who answers Mira's dawn pounding on her door with "Oh boy… What are you up to now, Mira Roehart?" and lets herself be dragged into the forest anyway; who perches on a mist-wet boulder with charcoal and parchment while Mira leaps naked from the top of a waterfall; who once left offerings for forest spirits, wove flower crowns for the nymphs and whispered wishes to the dryads in the oldest oaks — with Mira, when they both still believed.

Then Lucien Altheris spoke on the pier, and Kat's hazel-green eyes, "usually pools of quiet empathy, shone with feverish light." Baptized before the story begins, she brings Mira the news of "true Light" like a gift, and every conversation after that is a gentle, loving, relentless pull toward the altar. She calls the Wild Gods "demonic creatures… whispers of chaos and deceit," insists there is "no such thing as too much good," and makes the friendship itself the price: "I don't want to have to… stop talking to you… just because you haven't joined yet."

She never means it cruelly. That is the tragedy. Kat's faith is sincere, her joy at Mira's Baptism real, her "What an honor!" at the marriage proposal wholly innocent of what the marriage is. And when she is herself "chosen to relocate" to dangerous Blackwater Falls, the mask slips long enough for Mira to see the frightened girl beneath: "I know. I know it is… we can't just say no, can we?"

Mira, why don't you become one of The Chosen? Why don't you do the Baptism ritual? Just try it. Please. Be one of us.
— Kat at the Whispering Falls (The Chosen, Chapter 3)
Story in Progress
The Chosen is published in full. Kat leaves its pages on the western road; where the road takes her, and whether the soul-sisters meet again, belongs to The Forsaken. This profile keeps to the published book and the two public chapters of its sequel.

The Heart That Chose Certainty Over Questions

The author's own note on Kat calls her a "gentle artist" turned "zealous missionary," and the book shows both at once — the same hands that fuss over the wrinkles in Mira's wedding dress carved the wooden starling that Mira will carry into exile.

Shared Wonder
"You used to believe in the Wild Gods too. Remember? We both did." Kat's answer: "That was before. Before I found the truth. Before Zenith's Light illuminated the shadows" (Ch. 3).
The Steady Anchor
Calm, patient, the one person "who truly saw her without trying to reshape what she found" — until the reshaping became her mission (Ch. 2).
Zealous Faith
"Zenith's Light makes sense. More than anything else ever has." She dreams of being a Messenger of the Light, of carrying the crusade beyond Rivenglade — "the adventure," with divine purpose (Ch. 3, 6–7).
Conditional Love
"We're soul-sisters, Mira. Always and forever. We're meant to do this together. Aren't we?" — spoken in the same breath as the warning that she may have to stop speaking to her (Ch. 6).
I've felt such peace since my Baptism. Such purpose. It washed away all my anxieties, all my fears. It could change you too. I know it could.
— Kat to Mira (The Chosen, Chapter 3)

Hands That Shape Beauty and Belief

Kat has no magic. What she has is a satchel, a sketchbook, a carving knife in her past, and a faith strong enough to walk toward the Godscarred Wastes.

Creative Expression
The Sketchbook: Parchment and charcoal come out of her worn leather satchel at the falls, "fingers already itching to capture the raw beauty" (Ch. 3)
The Wooden Starling: A small bird "its wings outstretched in perpetual flight," carved by Kat long ago and given as a parting gift: "So you'll remember me… wherever you are" (Ch. 15)
Its Echo: As children Mira had given Kat a starling just like it — the birds are the two girls' oldest shared language
Its Journey: Mira packs it in her satchel the night she runs and cradles it in the Forsaken sanctuary (Ch. 21; Forsaken Ch. 24)
Spiritual Devotion
Baptized Early: Already one of the Chosen when the story opens; note-taker, elbow-nudger, mingler after sermons (Ch. 2–3)
Missionary Calling: Seizes on Lucien's crusade at once — "It's an adventure, Mira. Not just any adventure—the adventure" (Ch. 6)
Knows the Ranks: Faithful Elders, Devout Overseers, Messengers of the Light — and prays she and Mira will be "deemed worthy" together (Ch. 7)
Wedding Attendant: Adjusts Mira's white-and-gold gown "with eager hands," blind to the trembling beneath (Ch. 13)
Empathetic Connection
Comfort: Rubs small circles into Mira's back on the pier when the "overwhelmed" bride cannot speak (Ch. 12)
Blind Spots: "You're not… Forsaken, are you?" — the only danger she can imagine (Ch. 12)
Persuasive Care: Every push toward Zenith is framed as a wish for Mira's peace
Real Tears: "This isn't goodbye, Mira. It can't be." She breaks first, and hugs hardest (Ch. 15)
Chosen to Relocate
Destination: Blackwater Falls, a village west of Rivenglade "clinging to the fringes of civilization… on the threshold of the Godscarred Wastes—north of Solara's ruined bones" (Ch. 15)
The Danger: "Anything around Solara saw the worst of the Silence. Hordes of void-corrupted Beastborn hit the region"
Her Own Words: "I will be protected. Protected by Zenith's Light. I will help them rebuild."
Whose Doing: Sent the same day as Elias — Lucien calls it "a strategic decision" (Ch. 16)

From Soul-Sister to Zealous Convert

Kat's arc in The Chosen is short and complete: from the pier to the wagon in sixteen chapters.

The Journey from Light to Extremism
Before
Two girls in the woods, flower crowns and offerings and wooden birds; the "gentle, grounded friend" who was Mira's one exception in a town of failed interactions.
"Did you truly hear the Son of Zenith speak?" (Ch. 1)
On the pier, "the Kat she knew was not there" — only "a blank canvas of unyielding faith."
The Whispering Falls (Ch. 3)
Kat sketches, Mira swims; balance is argued against purity; Kat takes both of Mira's hands and asks her to be Baptized. Mira agrees to talk to Lucien — a lie that "felt heavier… than the deepest depths of the hidden pool."
The Condition (Ch. 6–8)
Kat volunteers for the crusade and names the price of friendship. She is in the front row, glowing, when Mira takes the altar; she cheers when Lucien proclaims her Chosen.
"What an honor!" (Ch. 12–13)
On the still pier she hears of the proposal and gasps, "Oh, stars, Mira!" She dresses the bride. She never sees the cage.
Farewells (Ch. 15–16)
Bags on the floor. Blackwater Falls. Fear under the faith. A wooden starling wrapped in twine. "We will see each other again. And we'll have so many stories to tell." Her wagon shrinks into the western road as Elias's sail vanishes north.
The Price of Certainty
Kat is what Zenith's Light does to a good heart. Nothing in her is destroyed — her kindness, her steadiness, her hunger to serve are all still there — but every one of them has been pointed at the altar. She loves Mira enough to weep at leaving her and not enough to hear her; she is frightened enough of Blackwater Falls to admit it and faithful enough to go. In Mira's satchel, a small carved bird keeps flying.
I know our adventures aren't turning out like we dreamed. But everything is going to be okay. It has to be.
— Kat, holding Mira for the last time in Rivenglade (The Chosen, Chapter 15)

The Tragedy of Lost Innocence

Katalina Petari is the first casualty of The Chosen, and she never bleeds. She is standing right beside Mira on the pier when Lucien speaks — and she is already gone, her quiet empathy replaced by "that unsettling, blissful serenity spreading through the marketplace like a gentle tide." Everything that follows between the two girls is Mira reaching for a friend who is reaching, just as lovingly, for a convert.

The book is careful never to make her a villain. Kat is the anchor who let Mira be strange; the artist with charcoal-black fingers; the girl who carved a starling to match the one she was given. Her faith gives her genuine peace and genuine purpose, and it costs her, one small step at a time, the ability to hear the person she loves most: she takes "What an honor!" for the truth of a proposal that was a sentence, and dresses her soul-sister for a wedding that is a funeral.

Her own ending in Rivenglade is a mirror of Mira's. She too is "chosen," she too cannot say no, and beneath the white robes she is a frightened young woman being sent toward the ruins of Solara "into the very jaws of whatever horrors lurked beyond Zenith's reach." For one moment on the floor among her bags the mask cracks, and Mira sees "the girl she had loved like a sister, trembling beneath the weight of expectation."

Then she is a silhouette on the western road, and a wooden bird in Mira's palm. Mira carries that bird out of Lucien's house, through the burning town, into the Forsaken's cavern, and prays for Kat's survival "in the far reaches of Blackwater Falls." Whether the prayer is answered — and what the soul-sisters would say to one another now — is a matter for The Forsaken.
