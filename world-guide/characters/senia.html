Light Reborn in Endless Dawn

In the stone-paved market square of Quenos, where morning light turns the sea mist to gold as it rises from the harbour, there is a woman with auburn hair who arranges peaches with careful, loving hands. She does not know that she is Senia, the sun incarnate, goddess of warmth and beauty and growing things. To herself she is a fruit vendor with a comfortable rhythm of banter for a fair-haired stranger who favours her peaches above all other market goods, and a warmth in her voice that she reserves only for him—though she would never admit it aloud.

She is one of the Wild Gods, and she is under a curse. Stripped of divinity and memory, she is reborn mortal, life after life, while Elodias—god of the moon and tides, her beloved since before the land was called Quenos—retains his immortality and his perfect recall and finds her again. Each finding gives them seven perfect days; then the sun sets on the seventh night, and fate claims its due. The Historian Tanneus G'Raja, who found on a windswept coast north of old Quenos a salt-crystallised journal full of her faces, believes he held "the grief of a god who remembers every life she lived, and every time she loved him—before the end came again."

Yet the tale is not only his grief. It is her sun. In every incarnation the same essential fire persists—the generous spirit that gives warmth without reservation, the practical humour, the courage to ask a stranger "Why me?"—and in every incarnation she is the reason he cannot stay away.

Why me? You could talk to any of them.
— Senia to Elodias, at her stall (Seven Days of Sun, Chapter 1)

The Radiant Heart

Whether crowned or barefoot, whether she is selling peaches or serving strangers beneath a creaking tavern sign, certain things about Senia do not change. The Historian's journal shows her face across dozens of lives—each different, each the same—and the woman of Quenos wears it with an unassuming warmth that other market girls envy and cannot imitate.

Naturally Warm
She saves the best peach without ever admitting she does. The banter over three coppers is "the comfortable rhythm of their daily dance," and her challenge—"Then go find another vendor"—holds no bite.
Curious, Then Drawn
First merely curious about the quiet stranger, then intrigued by his soft-spoken manner, then drawn to him in ways she cannot articulate even to herself.
Quietly Brave
She finds "an unexpected courage in her voice" to ask why he ignores every other woman in the square—and, when the answer strips away her defences, she pushes the coppers back and says the peach is his.
Willing
Offered a trade—the peach for a date—she nods once. "Yes." She feels something beginning: "Something real. Something strange. Something that tasted like love and danger all at once."
She felt like something was beginning. Something real. Something strange. Something that tasted like love and danger all at once.
— Seven Days of Sun, Chapter 1

From Innocence to Remembrance

Each of Senia's lives begins fresh, unmarked by the accumulated grief that Elodias carries for both of them. The Historian describes the connection between them as "recognition without memory, familiarity without origin"—and the movement from that recognition to full remembrance is the arc of every incarnation, and of the tale.

Lives Across Lifetimes

The journal the Historian found held dozens of women. "One with sunlit curls. One veiled in market cloth. One crowned in blossoms. Faces drawn in reverent detail—captured as if remembered from dreams. No names remained, but whoever drew them knew her. All of her." The tale itself turns a few more of its pages: the same burning auburn hair, the same hazel-green eyes that catch the sun, in life after life.

Seven Days of Sun

Each cycle follows the same arc, and the story's own summary states its shape plainly: each lifetime brings seven perfect days before fate claims its due. The chapters now online carry the reader as far as the trade of a peach for a date; the rest is the tale.

The Pattern of Love and Loss
The Stranger
A tall, fair-haired man begins buying her peaches. Curiosity becomes intrigue becomes something she cannot name. Other market girls watch with envy.
The Question
"Why me?" His answer—"Why would I look at the stars, when I have always loved the sun?"—settles over her "like the morning heat—slow and warm, spreading inward until it filled every corner of her chest."
The Trade
A peach for a date; the shore path when the sun starts to set. "Don't bring anything. Just you."
The Seven Days
Seven perfect days of love. What she comes to remember, and what her mortal form can bear of it, is the tale's to tell.
Beyond: Rebirth Awaits
The sun sets on the seventh night. Somewhere, a child is born with auburn hair; the moon begins to search again.

The Sun That Always Rises

Senia is the sun incarnate, goddess of warmth and beauty and growing things—and, in the life the Historian's tale opens upon, a fruit vendor in the market of Quenos who saves the best peach for a stranger with eyes the colour of a cloudless summer sky. She is one of the Wild Gods, bound by a curse woven from a lunar seer's jealousy: reborn mortal without memory of what she is, found by Elodias in every incarnation, loved for seven perfect days, and lost when the sun sets on the seventh night.

What makes her poignant is her innocence. Unlike Elodias, who carries perfect memory across every cycle, she begins each life fresh, and each life the same essential fire burns through—in a queen who does not wait to speak, in a tavern woman smiling through her weariness, in a barefoot dancer, a soldier, a painter, and a woman arranging peaches with careful, loving hands.

Whether her love is worth what it costs is a question the tale puts to her, not to him; the answer it gives her is in Seven Days of Sun. The star-readers, for their part, have already written it in the sky: two lights circling eternally, one remembering, one forgetting.

Follow the Sun's Journey

Meet Senia at her stall in the market of Quenos, and walk with her along the shore path at sunset. Her story explores love that survives death, innocence renewed, and the price of divine connection.
