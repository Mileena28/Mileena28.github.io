The Scholar Without Credentials

Alaquinn Althaea is a young elven woman who spent nearly all her savings on a beautiful blank journal, stared at it for half a morning, changed her ink three times, and then committed its first page to the one subject she could not stop thinking about: nature spirits. Every village has its stories of helpful brownies and mischievous pixies, every forest its dryads, every stream its water spirits — and yet, as she notes, actual scholarship on them is practically nonexistent. She suspects she knows why. Since the Divine War ended and the Wild Gods retreated into their hidden places, since prayers began echoing unanswered into the void, the learned have been too distracted by the Great Withdrawal to notice what remains. The spirits — Leika's children, if the oldest texts are to be believed — are still here. Still present, in ways their creators no longer are.

She has no field training, no institutional backing and no proper methodology, and she says so on the first page. Her tutors taught her languages and literature and basic magical theory; "How to Approach Potentially Dangerous Magical Creatures for Academic Purposes" was not part of the curriculum. What she has instead is curiosity, determination, and a really excellent journal — and a rumour of a village two days' ride north where the well water has turned bitter, the crops will not grow, and the locals blame a "poisoned spring spirit." She goes to see for herself.

What she finds at Elden Ridge turns amateur documentation into something far more urgent. A water sprite that kept a town's water clean for thirty years has forgotten what it was for; when she finally coaxes it to speak, it warns her, in a voice not its own, that "The Silence… It's coming…" This is Voices of the Wild: a research journal that begins as cozy academic comedy and, entry by entry, brings its keeper closer to truths that should have remained buried — where, as the author's blurb puts it, the very act of seeking knowledge might be hastening the world's undoing.

The pressure of a pristine journal is almost paralyzing... What could possibly be worthy of such perfect, unmarked potential?
— Day 1, the first entry

Earnest to a Fault

Quinn's journal is the record of a sharp, observant mind housed in a hopelessly unpolished researcher. She notices what matters — that the sacred animals moved their dens upstream a moon ago, that a sprite's giggle can go suddenly hollow — while forgetting to ask the name of the warden who has been helping her for two days running. She documents her own disasters with the same care she gives her sprite sketches, and signs each entry with a title that is half boast and half apology.

Infectious Enthusiasm
"I made it! And I already have ACTUAL RESEARCH!" — written on feet covered in blisters, arms covered in bug bites, after three days of walking. Failure dampens her for about a paragraph.
Endearing Incompetence
Packs no offerings, no insect repellent and the wrong boots; never thinks to research local regulations; tastes the town's bitter well water in full view of an elderly woman and makes "the most dramatic grimace imaginable."
Authentic to the Bone
Calls out "Please come forth, little sprite!" to empty air while villagers whisper about moonleaf; shouts "The Silence is COMING!" at the municipal officials as they push her out the door; tells the closed door, "You'll regret this!"
Self-Deprecating Humour
Signs herself "Aspiring Scholar of... Something," "Keeper of Questions I Don't Know How to Answer Yet," "Tired but Triumphant Field Researcher," "Temporarily Banned but Newly Hopeful Researcher."
Genuine Insight
Connects the warden's restless blessed deer and marked foxes, the two-moon timeline of the bitter water, and the sprite's forgotten purpose into a single question no one else in Elden Ridge is asking.
Scholarly Tunnel Vision
"Really must remember to get names at introductions." Learns the warden's name on Day 6 — from a passing secretary — after agreeing to a second meeting with him.
I have absolutely no idea how to begin... I don't have institutional backing or formal training or even a proper methodology. But that's not going to stop me.
— Day 1

From Amateur to Alarm-Raiser

The first six days of the journal take Quinn from a desk and a blank page to a municipal hall's slammed door. Her methods are unorthodox — public humiliation, bureaucratic violations, the sacrifice of a once-in-a-lifetime skipping stone — but they work, and what they uncover is not the misbehaviour of one sprite but the shape of a larger crisis.

Research Timeline: Six Days of Discovery
Day 1 — The Beginning of Everything
Spends nearly all her savings on the journal. Decides to study nature spirits because "actual scholarship" on them "is practically nonexistent." Sets out for a village two days' ride north with bitter wells and failing crops.
Day 4 — First Field Research at Elden Ridge
Three days of walking; blisters on blisters. Interviews a young warden of the Ridge (blessed deer and marked foxes restless "about a moon ago") and an older man shouting at the well: the water sprite kept the water crystal clear for thirty years in exchange for bread and honey cakes, and for nearly two moons has done nothing but squirt people and giggle.
Day 5 — First Contact with Water Sprite
Tastes the well water ("like it had been sitting in a rusty bucket"). Offers her perfect skipping stone; is ignored, then spat on. The warden hands her a silver piece — "You can't go wrong with something shiny" — and the sprite comes for it. It does not remember what it was supposed to do. Then, in a hollow, distant voice: "The Silence... It's coming..."
Day 6 — Official Correspondence, and a Disaster
A cease-and-desist notice from the Elden Ridge Municipal Authority (Statute 847-B; fifteen silver pieces a day; "gentle but firm physical escort"). She marches to the Municipal Hall to explain the gravity of her findings and is pushed out the door mid-sentence.
Day 6 — An Unexpected Lifeline
Slumped outside the hall, she is found by the warden — Dani, as she finally learns — who brings her a notice from the Hollow Spire Scholarly Consortium, offers to help her write her application, and asks to meet by the river at dusk. What they write there, and where it takes her, is the matter of the entries to come.
The Silence... It's coming...
— The water sprite, Day 5

Uncovering the Crisis

Quinn's field notes from Elden Ridge are a first-hand record of a nature spirit in the years before the Silence — and a documented warning of what was coming.

Sprite Behavioural Changes
Abandonment of Duty: thirty years of faithful water-keeping ended nearly two moons ago
Memory Loss: "I don't even remember what I was supposed to do"
Compulsive Pranking: squirting villagers, then giggling "like wind chimes"
Physical Description: eight to ten inches tall; chubby, translucent blue-green body with silvery highlights; enormous deep blue-green eyes holding "tiny points of light"; a "water mane" that flows around its neck independent of any breeze; small webbed fingers
"The Silence" Phenomenon
The Warning: the sprite went still and spoke in "a voice completely different from before — hollow, distant"
Its Effect: a spirit that has forgotten its fundamental purpose and "doesn't see the point" of it
Timeline: the water turned about two moons ago; the sacred animals grew restless about one moon ago
Her Question: are other spirits experiencing the same changes and memory loss?
Research Methodology
Direct Observation: empirical water tasting; frantic charcoal sketches
Interview Technique: walking up to the first elf in the square and asking about the wells
Offering Experiments: skipping stone (failed); silver piece placed on the streambed where it catches the light (success)
Pattern Recognition: animal behaviour, water quality and sprite behaviour set against a single timeline
Where the Journal Goes
Day 1: "I want to understand the nature spirits — not just catalog them like specimens, but actually know them."
Days 4–5: no training, no credentials, no methodology; persistence through failure and public embarrassment
Day 5: the crucial discovery — a sprite that has forgotten its purpose, and a warning about "The Silence"
Day 6: municipal authorities dismiss the warning and ban the research
Day 6: the Hollow Spire Scholarly Consortium — "a sanctuary for learning" seeking scholars "willing to pursue truth even when it challenges conventional wisdom" — offers a way forward
Beyond: her research will carry her far past Elden Ridge, and what she brings to the Hollow Spire — and whom — will cost her, and the world, more than she can know

Connections Made Along the Way

The Power of Passionate Amateurism

Alaquinn Althaea begins her journal by asking why nature spirits exist at all in a world where the gods have grown silent — whether they are simply Leika's children meant to tend the wild places, teachers of harmony with the natural world, or guardians of secrets too ancient for mortal minds. She wants to know them, not catalogue them. "After all," she writes, "if we can't hear the gods anymore, maybe we need to learn to hear the stones."

Her first expedition is a comedy of small disasters that turns, in the space of a single afternoon, into something else. The sprite of Elden Ridge is exactly as enchanting as she hoped — "like someone took the concept of a tiny dragon and reimagined it as a creature made of water and joy" — and it is also wrong in a way that frightens her. It has forgotten its one job. It does not see the point. And when she asks why, something ancient and troubled looks out of its enormous eyes and names a thing that fills it with dread: the Silence.

Quinn understands at once that she is out of her depth. Who does she tell? Does she write a formal report, and to whom? The answer Elden Ridge gives her is a cease-and-desist order and a door slammed in her face — the first, but not the last, institution to treat her warning as the raving of an unauthorised researcher. The answer Dani gives her is a silver piece, a fellowship notice, and an evening by the river.

Her voice — self-deprecating, observant, swinging between dramatic despair and infectious enthusiasm — makes the journal as entertaining as it is unsettling. She records her failures alongside her findings, her research priorities alongside her hair, and the reader watches a researcher find her method and her nerve at the same time.

Where the journal goes from Elden Ridge is the substance of Voices of the Wild. It is enough to say here what the author has said: that this is a prequel to The Silence, that each entry brings Quinn closer to truths that should have remained buried, and that the very act of seeking knowledge might be hastening the world's undoing. Her generosity — the assumption that knowledge-seekers belong in knowledge-seeking places — will cost her, and the world, more than she can know. And this is not the last the world will hear of Alaquinn Althaea.
