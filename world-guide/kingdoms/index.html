Major Kingdoms

The great realms where crowned heads rule over territories shaped by divine intervention and cosmic consequence

Kingdom of Atheria

Breadbasket Kingdom

A human kingdom of rolling, fertile plains inland from the Azure Expanse, north of Rivenglade, whose modern cities gleam above the cliffs of old Quenos, the seaside village where gods once walked beside mortals; its very name, "carried from the sea," recalls a great wave that destroyed the old town generations ago. Long the "breadbasket kingdom" of golden wheat and barley, ruled by a pragmatic royal lineage from its bustling coastal capital of Freeport, Atheria served as neutral ground for the diplomacy of the realms. The Silence left its cities standing but its heartland blighted by Beastborn and void-corruption, so that a resilient, industrious people now lean on their fishing fleets while they struggle to reclaim their fields—and Zenith's Chosen slowly encroaches.

Kingdom of Solara

Golden Realm (in Ruins)

Solara is a southern kingdom associated with divine fire, white stone, gold, and the Solarius royal house. In The Silence, Dain defeats Valerius in the palace and claims the throne; Valerius leaves alive with loyal guards. Dain encourages persecution before admitting Verites into his body. The royal crown taken in the coup is separate from the sealed Crown of Aetheris, which Verites later uses to amplify the occupied body. After Dain’s death and Aetheris’s destruction, civilians begin rebuilding, but the novel names no successor. The Chosen later depicts refugees from Solara in Rivenglade. The older history of Moradim belongs to The Lost Histories. In the stories: The Silence, Chapters 29, 31, 38, 43–44; The Chosen, Chapter 1.

Sommer Isle

Elven Paradise

A secluded tropical island paradise in the Azure Expanse, southeast of Atheria, where the Sommer Isle Elves keep strict social hierarchies, a deep-seated sense of spiritual superiority, and near-exclusive devotion to Zenith—a structured, intellectual worship they believe preserved untainted by the Wild God pantheon. Their pristine white marble and gold cities and their disdain for the "low elves" of the Verdant Veil mask the dangerous seduction of a flawed utopian vision that prioritizes order over growth. Lucien Altheris was raised here. Before the Divine War, these were the eastern islands of Fayespire.

Dawnseekers of the Verdant Veil

Nature-Guided Realm

The Verdant Veil's reclusive elven civilization—stewards of the wild who preserve the delicate balance between nature and magic and hear the whispers of the Wild Gods in leaf and stream. A royal house carries Dawnseeker diplomacy to the human kingdoms, as Princess Elowen Faerwyn did on the eve of the Silence.

The Reclamation Zone

Military Government (Age of Gods)

In the Age of Gods, a war-torn region known as the Reclamation Zone was held under the military authority of Governor Verin—a sharp-eyed woman whose rise was built on unflinching pragmatism and ruthless discipline—who demanded documented identity and passage papers of strangers, dispatched agents to observe them, and convened a religious council of recognized consistories quick to name an undocumented healer a heresiarch. She saw herself as a bulwark against hidden contagions—ideologies, creeds, mythologies—that might unravel her hard-won post-war order, and the Zone is remembered chiefly for what it did to the wandering god who healed its people.

Kingdom of Moradim (later Solara)

Kingdom of the Age of Gods · Before the Divine War

Before the Divine War, the land that is now Solara was the Kingdom of Moradim—a wealthy mainland kingdom of trade routes and mainland connections, whose alliances were secured by diplomatic marriage. Its royal heir, Princess Jade, was betrothed to a prince of the eastern islands of Fayespire (the Vaelari islands that would one day be called Sommer Isle), whose people needed Moradim's wealth and mainland connections. Moradim's wealth would prove able to purchase truth itself; how murder became mythology on its High Pass road is told in The Song of Blood and Stone. What the Divine War did over Moradim, and why the kingdom that rose from it bears the Sun God's name, is a matter for The Divine War.

Imperial Republic of Hevetica

Empire Across the Sea · From a Work in Development

Across the sea from Arcadia lies the Imperial Republic of Hevetica—a nation of towering, long-lived Bohdkin whose gods never left them, whose weapons are blessed, and whose seers are infused with divine magic through ritual. Militant, patriotic, and strictly ordered, they believe their order is superior. They believe Arcadia—a land that has nearly ended the world more than once—cannot be trusted to govern itself. So, in the Age of Occupation after The Song of the Dragon, they come to govern it. Senach is their word for an Arcadian: godless savage. Hevetica belongs to The Senach, a series in development; everything here is premise, and subject to change.

Religious-Political Movements

Where faith becomes the foundation of power and divine mandate shapes governmental authority

Zenith's Chosen

A monotheistic movement that rose in Rivenglade five years after the Silence under Lucien Altheris, the self-proclaimed "Son of Zenith," seeking to replace Wild God worship with absolute submission to Zenith's Light. Its hierarchy of Devout Overseers, Faithful Elders, and baptized Chosen combines spiritual authority with political control through mandatory sermons, systematic conversion, family surveillance, and the Forsaking of dissidents—and its missionaries now reach into Atheria and toward the Godscarred Wastes.

Study the Movement

Forsaken Resistance Networks

Underground communities of those cast out and branded by Zenith's Chosen, sheltering in a ruined temple of the Wild Gods under pragmatic, survival-focused leadership. Their hidden sanctuary and their resistance operations—including the blast that tore open Rivenglade's Zenithian Hall—challenge absolute religious authority through solidarity and strategic action.

Tribal Confederations

Societies organized around strength, survival, and adaptation to the harshest environments

Bloodfang Clans

Nomadic berserker tribes of the Crimson Tundra—an alliance of Ursine, Lupine, Avian, and Feline Beastmen, each species keeping its own tribe under Bloodfang law—ruled by strength and savagery and bound by devotion to the War God. Their culture represents survival through embracing rather than avoiding the world's harsh realities, following the migrations of tundra beasts in a cycle of hunting, raiding, and blood-soaked ritual, including the sacrifice of Bloodmarked children beneath the Blood Moon.

Join the Hunt

Ashborn Tribes

Nomadic scavenger tribes of the Godscarred Wastes who worship the remnants of the fallen gods and hunt for fragments of divine relics on the ancient battlefields, trading them like coin. Their pragmatic society adapted to supernatural threats through fragmented tribal structures, relic hunting, hard-won knowledge of surviving divinely corrupted territories, and a deep fear of the Bloodmarked, whom their war-songs remember as a curse. What the Ashborn were before the Wastes were scarred is a question for The Divine War.

Walk the Wastes

Specialized Organizations

Unique institutions of knowledge that arose in response to supernatural challenges

Chroniclers of Bone

Scholars and mystics of the Hollow Spire, dedicated to uncovering the mysteries of the gods and, in the days of High Inquisitor Mordain Ashcroft, to combating the lingering stain of divine influence through systematic documentation and increasingly militant approaches to magical phenomena.
